## 4.

In Germany the return to Kant dates from Liebmann’s book on _Kant und die Epigonen_, 1865, and F. A. Lange’s _Geschichte des Materialismus_, 1866.
Cf. Hans Vaihinger, _Hartmann, Dühring und Lange_, Iserlohn, 1876, S. 18 f., 54 f., usw.; Ed. von Hartmann, _Neukantianismus, Schopenhauerianismus und Hegelianismus_, 2e Aufl., 1877, S. 1-118.
In theology Kantianism was revived by Ritschl and Lipsius.
The _principia_ of the theology of Ritschl and his school are these: first of all, a separation must be made between metaphysics and religion (theology).
The metaphysical concept of the Absolute has nothing to do with the religious idea of God, Ritschl, _Theol. u. Metaph._, 1881, S. 13; _Rechtf. u. Vers._ III² 201 f., 211 f., 221 f.
Herrmann, _Die Religion im Verhältniss zum Welterkennen und zur Sittlichkeit_, Halle, 1879, carried the separation still further and distinguished between the object of metaphysics and that of religion, ib., S. 100; between the _erklärbare_ and the _erlebbare Wirklichkeit_, S. 100, 107; and between the natural and the moral world, S. 269, 355.
Cf. also Kaftan, _Das Wesen der christl. Religion_, Basel, 1881, S. 200.
Next, theology must take its standpoint not prior to faith, in a _theologia naturalis_ that does not exist, but in the faith of the congregation.
From there it must proceed and view and evaluate everything, Ritschl, _Theol. u. Metaph._, S. 11 f., 61; _Rechtf. u. Vers._ I² 364, III 2-8, 13, 32, 162, 165, 198, 304, 356 f.
Orthodoxy did indeed allow the _fides historica_, the _assensus_, to precede the _fides salvifica_, that is, the _fiducia_, but this conflicted with the original doctrine of the Reformation in Luther, Melanchthon, and Calvin.
In that way faith would continually depend on external proofs, consist in accepting certain truths, and be robbed of all freedom, independence, and certainty, Ritschl, _Fides implicita_, Bonn, 1890, S. 69; Gottschick, _Die Kirchlichkeit der sogenannten kirchl. Theol._, Freiburg, 1890, S. 11-53, 69 f.; Traub, _Glaube u. Theol._, _Stud. u. Krit._ 1893, 3tes Heft, S. 568-588; Herrmann, _Die Gewissheit des Glaubens und die Freiheit der Theol._, Freiburg, 1887, 2e Aufl., 1889; id., _Der Verkehr des Christen mit Gott_, Stuttgart, 1886, S. 18 f., 83 f., 92 f., 2e Aufl., 1892; id., _Der evang. Glaube und die Theol. Albr. Ritschls_, 1890; id., in _Beweis des Glaubens_, 1890; Kaftan, _Brauchen wir ein neues Dogma_, 1890, S. 37; id., _Glaube u. Dogma_, _Zeits. f. Theol. u. Kirche v. Gottschick_ 1891, S. 478 f.; cf. 1893, S. 427 f.; Kattenbusch, _Ueber religiösen Glauben im Sinne des Christ._, Giessen, 1887.
In the third place, Ritschl’s theology seeks to bind saving faith to the historical revelation in the person of Christ.
Faith, precisely as _fiducia_, is not the acceptance of a doctrine but trust in a person.
But then faith naturally does not arise of itself from within the human being; it presupposes an object and some prior knowledge of that object.
Herrmann even acknowledges that the _notitia_ is a _Vorbedingung des Glaubens_, _Verkehr des Christen mit Gott_, 2e Aufl., S. 182; _Zeits. f. Theol. u. K. von Gottschick_ IV, 4tes Heft, S. 273.
On what ground, then, does that object, that historical revelation in Christ, stand firm?
Whence comes certainty concerning its reality?
The answers given by the Ritschlians to that important question differ greatly.
Ritschl himself really gives no answer.
He directs the individual to the congregation, which has the experience of the forgiveness of sins and of being children of God, but does not say what must induce the individual person to join that congregation.
He only grounds the existence of God and the reasonableness of the Christian worldview in the spirit’s self-appraisal, _Rechtf. u. Vers._ III² 200 f., 209 f., 573 f.
His followers, however, usually take other paths and make the truth of the revelation in Christ rest upon inner experience.
The contemplation of Christ affects us through his moral greatness; he makes a deep impression on the unprejudiced mind, humbles it, but also raises it to the confidence that God is our Father, Herrmann, _Der Verkehr des Christen mit Gott_, 1886, S. 18 f., 26 f.; _Beweis des Glaubens_, 1890, S. 81 f.; Gottschick, _Die Kirchlichkeit der sogen. kirchl. Theol._, 1890, S. 222, 226; Traub, _Stud. u. Krit._ 1893, S. 577; cf. Köstlin, _Die Begründung unserer sittl. relig. Ueberzeugung_, 1893, S. 98 f.; Frank, _Dogmatische Studien_, 1892, S. 46 f.
Herrmann denies as strongly as possible that historical proofs can convince us of the truth of the person of Christ; he also leaves historical criticism completely free and withholds nothing from it, yet he says that the Christ _extra nos_ is the ground of his faith, _Zeits. f. Theol. u. K._ 1894, IV, 4tes Heft, S. 281.
He speaks of a _zweifellose Thatsache_, S. 280; holds that the basic features of the image of Jesus permit no doubt concerning the historical reality of Jesus, S. 285; and acknowledges this objective truth not on the ground of proofs but because he has felt and experienced the power of Jesus’s life over him, S. 288; because Jesus’s life has the power _sich unserm Gewissen als eine wirkliche Thatsache zu bezeugen_, S. 293.
And only that constitutes the content of faith which has the power _sich dem Gewissen als wirkliche Thatsache zu bezeugen_, S. 293.
The school of Ritschl gives us no deeper analysis of the truth and certainty of faith; the impression that the person of Christ makes upon us, the power that proceeds from the gospel in our heart, the moral experience of humility and joy, and the agreement of the idea of the kingdom of God with our needs and demands, Kaftan, _Die Wahrheit der chr. Relig._, Basel, 1889, S. 537 f., seem to guarantee the truth of revelation in the person and life of Christ.
Finally, the content of faith is reduced to a minimum in Ritschl’s theology.
Ritschl said, _Rechtf. u. Vers._ III² 369; cf. 191 f.: _alle Erkenntnisse religiöser Art sind directe Werthurtheile_.
Philosophy also evaluates.
But there the _Werthurtheile_ are incidental and accompanying.
In religion they are independent; they rest upon the human being’s pleasure or displeasure, upon his weal or woe; they concern his relation to the world and are independent of science.
Thus, if science remains within its own territory and does not attempt to construct a worldview, no conflict is possible, Ritschl, _Theol. u. Metaph._ 7, 9; _Rechtf. u. Vers._ III² 189-197; _Fides implicita_, S. 68, 70.
Herrmann went still further and even stated that the reality of faith was an illusion for science, _Die Religion usw._, S. 108, 112.
Nevertheless, Ritschl and Herrmann retained the historical revelation of God in Christ as the foundation and source of religious faith and, to that extent, made religion consist not only in _Werth-_ but also in _Seinsurtheile_.
It was also all too clear that religious knowing includes the conviction of the reality of its object just as much as scientific knowing does.
_Werthurtheile_ are dependent upon _Seinsurtheile_, or else they are nothing but illusion.
Kaftan, _Das Wesen der christl. Religion_, S. 35 f., 41 f., 55 f., therefore also acknowledges that the _Werthurtheile_ presuppose objective truth, even though in an _erkenntnisstheoretischen_ sense he also makes the latter depend upon the former.
And Häring, _Die Theol. und der Vorwurf der doppelten Wahrheit_, 1886, does say that practical, moral grounds move us to faith, but nevertheless adds that Christian faith does not carry within itself the guarantee for the truth of what is believed—for wishing is no proof of the fulfillment of the wish—but that it finds this guarantee in the historical fact of God’s revelation in Christ; cf. Häring also, _Zur Lehre von der h. Schrift_, _Stud. u. Krit._ 1893, 1es Heft, S. 177-212, especially S. 199 f.
Hence others point out that, however much in Scripture may succumb to criticism, enough nevertheless remains firm.
_Die ganze Erscheinung Christi_ cannot, after all, be unhistorical.
Faith is independent of the supernatural birth of Christ, Harnack, _Das apost. Glaubensbekenntniss_, Berlin, 1892; of the resurrection, Harnack, _Dogmengesch._ I² 73 f.; Gottschick, _Die Kirchlichkeit usw._, S. 90; and of the miracles.
Only if historical-critical investigation sought to deprive us of the conviction that Christ is our Lord would Christian faith rise in opposition to it; cf. Otto Ritschl, _Der histor. Christus, der christl. Glaube u. die theol. Wissenschaft_, _Zeits. f. Theol. u. Kirche v. Gottschick_ III 4, 5tes Heft, S. 371 f.; Traub, _Stud. u. Krit._ 1893, S. 577 f.; Erich Haupt, _Die Bedeutung der h. Schrift für den evang. Christen_, 1891; Max Reischle, _Der Glaube an Jesus Christus und die geschichtliche Erforschung seines Lebens_, Leipzig, 1893.

Ritschl_, 1888; Pfleiderer, _Die Ritschl’sche Theol._, _Jahrb. f. prot. Theol._, April 1889, S. 162 f., also issued separately at Braunschweig by Schwetske; id., on Herrmann, Kaftan, and Bender, _Jahrb. f. prot. Theol._ 1891, S. 321-383; id., _Entw. der prot. Theol._, 1891, S. 228 f.; Flügel, _Die spekul. Theol. der Gegenwart_, 1888, S. 230 f.; Frank, _Die Theol. A. Ritschl’s_, 3e Aufl., Erl., 1891; Flügel, _Ritschl’s philos. und theol. Ansichten_, 2e Aufl., 1892; G. Mielke, _Das System A. Ritschl’s dargestellt, nicht kritisirt_, Bonn, Marcus, 1894; Saussaye on Ritschl, _Theol. Stud. van Dr. Daubanton_, 1884, p. 259 f., and my article there, 1888, p. 369 f.
