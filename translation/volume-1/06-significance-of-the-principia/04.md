## 4.

Thus we have come to know three _principia_.
First, God as the _principium essendi_ of theology.
Next, the _principium cognoscendi externum_, namely, the self-revelation of God, which, insofar as it is deposited in Holy Scripture, bears an instrumental and temporary character.
And finally, the _principium cognoscendi internum_, the illumination of the human being by God’s Spirit.
These three are one in that they have God as their author and one and the same knowledge of God as their content.
The _theologia archetypa_ in the divine consciousness; the _theologia ectypa_, given in revelation and deposited in Holy Scripture; and the _theologia in subjecto_, the knowledge of God insofar as it passes from revelation into human consciousness and is received there—all three are from God.
It is God Himself who discloses His self-knowledge, communicates it through revelation, and introduces it into the human being.
They are also substantively one, for it is one and the same pure and true knowledge of God that He has of Himself, that He communicates in revelation, and that He introduces into human consciousness.
Therefore, they neither may nor can ever be separated and detached from one another.
On the other hand, they must nevertheless be carefully distinguished.
For the knowledge God has of Himself is absolute, simple, and infinite and, in its absoluteness, is incommunicable to finite consciousness.
Formerly, therefore, the _theologia archetypa_ was sometimes restricted to that part of God’s self-knowledge which He had resolved to communicate to creatures.
But this distinction makes the relation between _theologia archetypa_ and _theologia ectypa_ mechanical and forgets that absoluteness lies not only in quantity but also in quality.
Nevertheless, it contains the true insight that the _theologia ectypa_ bestowed upon creatures through revelation is not the absolute self-knowledge of God but that knowledge of God as it has been accommodated to and fitted for finite consciousness, and thus anthropomorphized.
This _theologia ectypa_, which lies objectively before us in revelation, is _externa_, but it is destined to be conveyed into the consciousness of rational creatures, to become _theologia ectypa interna_, _theologia in subjecto_; there, however, it also undergoes changes according to the nature of the subject.
It differs—not _re et ratione_, but nevertheless _gradu et modo_—in Christ (_theol. unionis_), in the angels and the blessed (_theol. visionis_), and in human beings on earth (_theol. viatorum, viae, revelationis_); and among the latter it differs again in prophets and apostles, theologians and laypeople.
In each person’s consciousness it is modified according to individual capacity.
Substantively, however, it is and remains one and the same knowledge, which proceeds from God and is transplanted by way of revelation into the consciousness of His rational creatures.
These three _principia_, distinct and yet essentially one, rest in the Trinitarian being of God.
It is the Father who, through the Son as Logos, communicates Himself in the Spirit to His creatures.
