## 35.

At the beginning of the nineteenth century Reformed theology was in grievous decline almost everywhere. In the form of Supranaturalism, theology was practiced in this country by Van der Palm, Van Voorst, Borger, Clarisse, Kist, and van Hengel at Leiden; by Abresch, Chevallier, Muntinghe, and Ypey at Groningen; by Heringa, Royaards, Bouman, and Vinke at Utrecht; by many able and prominent ministers—Dermout, Broes, Donker Curtius, van Senden, Egeling, and others; and by many essayists in the Stolpian legacy, 1756, Teyler’s Society, 1778, and the Hague Society, 1787. This supranaturalistic current wished to be rational, not rationalistic in the sense of Wegscheider, Röhr, and Paulus. It maintained revelation and argued for its necessity, possibility, and truth on all kinds of rational and historical grounds. It wished to be biblical and was anti-confessional, anti-philosophical, and anti-Calvinistic. It acquired a dogmatics that was deistic in theology, Pelagian in anthropology, moralistic in Christology, collegialistic in ecclesiology, and eudaemonistic in eschatology. In the northern provinces of the country it was replaced around 1835 by Groningen theology, which, following the Socratic philosophy of Van Heusde, d. 1839, replaced the idea of revelation and doctrine with that of education and thus incorporated an ethical element into the relation of God and man. Here God was not primarily Teacher but the great Educator who, through nature and history and through the person and church of Christ, trained human beings as His children to become wise and devout Christians, to conformity with God. The opposition it encountered from the orthodox side and soon also from the modern side, and its internal development toward the Evangelical current, caused it to give way around 1850 to Modern Theology. This arose with Opzoomer, who became professor at Utrecht in 1845 and applied the empirical philosophy of Mill and Comte to religion. He arrived at anti-supranaturalism. In his _Leer der Hervormde Kerk_, Scholten finally provided a modern dogmatics under the Reformed flag. And Kuenen appeared as an advocate of evolution in the field of Old Testament Scripture. Thus there was gradually a theological development toward unbelief; modern theology no longer has a dogmatics. Over against these negative currents, however, positive currents arose in our country during this century: those of the Réveil and the Secession, of the Utrecht and ethical-irenic schools, and finally of Calvinism, which strives to win a position of its own also in theology. See further my essay on the _Gesch. der theol. in Nederland_, _Presb. and Ref. Review_, April 1892, and _Tĳdschr. voor Geref. Theol._, June and July 1894, pp. 161 f., and the literature listed there. In Germany Reformed theology declined ever further. The Reformed church had conquered a broad territory in Germany. Its theology was practiced at Heidelberg, Duisburg, Marburg, Frankfurt a/O, Herborn, Bremen, and Halle. Thus it remained until the middle of the eighteenth century. But then came the _Aufklärung_, in 1817 the Union, followed by the influence of the philosophy of Kant, Schleiermacher, and others; and all these causes brought the Reformed church and theology in Germany into complete decline. At the beginning of this century there was indeed some awakening of Reformed consciousness as well, in Krafft at Erlangen, G. D. Krummacher at Elberfeld, Geibel at Lübeck, Mallet at Bremen, and others. Yet this was not strong enough. Even men such as Ebrard, d. 1888, at Erlangen and Heppe, d. 1879, at Marburg caused great harm to the Reformed cause through their Melanchthonianism. Only Wichelhaus, d. 1858, at Halle; Karl Südhoff, d. 1865, at Frankfurt a/M.; Böhl at Vienna; Dr. A. Zahn at Stuttgart; O. Thelemann at Detmold; Kohlbrugge, d. 1875, at Elberfeld; and others placed themselves decisively on the foundation of the Reformed confession. At present there is in Germany no longer a single theological university or school and not a single theological professor of Reformed confession. A. Zahn, _Die Ursachen des Niederganges der ref. Kirche in Deutschl._, Barmen, 1881; idem, _Abriss einer Gesch. der ev. Kirche auf dem europ. Festlande im 19 Jahrh._, Stuttgart, 3e Aufl., 1893, 12tes Kapitel.
