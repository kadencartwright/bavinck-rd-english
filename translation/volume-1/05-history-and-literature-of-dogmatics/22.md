## 22.

For Lutheran dogmatics in the nineteenth century, besides the works cited above, the following are relevant: Mücke, _Die Dogm. des 19 Jahrh._, Gotha, 1857; E. Lichtenberger, _Histoire des idées relig. en Allemagne_, Paris, Fischbacher; Thilo, _Die Wissenschaftlickkeit der modernen spekul. Theol. in ihren Principien beleuchtet_, Leipz., 1851; O. Flügel, _Die spekulative Theol. der Gegenwart_, 2e Aufl., Cöthen, 1888; Hagenbach, _Ueber die sogenannte Vermittelungstheologie_, 1858; Carl Schwarz, _Gesch. der neueren Theol._, 1857, Dutch translation by Krabbe; Hartmann, _Die Krisis des Christ. in der mod. Theol._, Berlin, 1880; O. Pfleiderer, _Die Entw. der prot. Theol. in Deutschland seit Kant und in Gross-Britt. seit 1825_, Freiburg, 1891; Frank, _Gesch. u. Krit. der neueren Theol. u. s. w._, herausgeg. von P. Schaarschmidt, Erl. Deichert, 1894; Kattenbusch, _von Schleiermacher zu Ritschl_, Giessen, 1892. For Scandinavia: _Presbyt. and Ref. Rev._, Oct. 1893. The distinctive feature of Lutheran dogmatics in this century is that it stands almost entirely under the influence of philosophy. In the previous century it had through Rationalism lost almost completely its principle, method, and content. It had become a compendium of rational thoughts about God, virtue, and immortality. Kant was the first who, through his sharp critique of pure reason, completely undermined this rational foundation of dogmatics. His critique was discouraging, even destructive, for the rationalism and eudaemonism of the _Aufklärung_. But through practical reason he attempted to regain what he had lost through the _reine Vernunft_. The categorical imperative, the moral consciousness, gives us the right to postulate the existence of God, freedom, and immortality. Dogmatics is built upon morality; religion becomes a means to virtue; God, an emergency aid for man. The content of religion and dogmatics, developed by Kant in his _Religion innerhalb der Grenzen der blossen Vernunft_, 1793, is purely rationalistic. Kant still stands wholly in the eighteenth century; the historical and positive have no value for him; he isolates man from all influences; only autonomous _Vernunftreligion_ is true religion. But through his critique of reason, through his appeal to the moral consciousness, and through his strict conception of morality, which even caused him to speak of the _radikal Böse_ in man and of the necessity of an _Art Widergeburt_, he exercised great influence on theology. Not only man’s moral destiny but also reason’s inability to reach the supersensible became an argument for the necessity of revelation, faith, and authority. In this Kantian spirit dogmatics was treated by Tieftrunk, _Dilucidationes ad theoreticam religionis christ. partem_, 1793; Stäudlin, J. E. C. Schmidt, Ammon, and others.
