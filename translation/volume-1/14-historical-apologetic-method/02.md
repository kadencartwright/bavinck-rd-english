## 2.

The Reformation did indeed oppose this hierarchy of Rome in principle and adopt another standpoint. It took its position not in natural reason, in order successively to lead this up to faith, but in Christian faith. And, as will be demonstrated later, it declared as decisively as possible that this faith rested solely on God’s authority and was wrought solely by the Holy Spirit. Protestant theologians, however, did not always maintain this principle strictly and repeatedly returned to the doctrine of _theologia naturalis_ and of the historical proofs for the truth of revelation. Calvin says, _Inst._ I, 7, 3, that it would be easy for him to prove the divinity of Holy Scripture, and in cap. 8 advances various grounds for it. Ursinus, _Tract. Theol._, pp. 1–33; Zanchius, _Op._ VIII, col. 335 sq.; Polanus, _Synt. Theol._ I, c. 17 sq., capp. 27, 28; _Synopsis pur. theol._, disp. 2, 10 sq.; Du Plessis-Mornay, _Traité de la vérité de la religion chrétienne contre les Athées_, etc., Antwerp, 1581; Abbadie, _Traité de la vérité de la religion chrét._, 1684, and so forth, speak and proceed in the same way. Cf. Heppe, _Dogm. der ev. ref. K._, pp. 20–22; Hase, _Hutt. Rediv._ §37; Schmid, _Dogm. der ev. luth. K._, pp. 32, 33. The conviction that these proofs were sufficient to produce at least a _fides humana_ involuntarily contributed to emancipating reason from faith and to placing the dogmas of _theologia naturalis_ and of Holy Scripture outside the _fides salvifica_. With this rationalism also began in the Protestant churches. Socinianism rejected the _testimonium Spiritus Sancti_ and grounded the truth of Christianity on historical proofs, _Catech. Racov._, qu. 5–30; Fock, _Der Socin._, 338 f. Remonstrantism took the same path, Episcopius, _Instit. Theol._, lib. IV, cap. 2; Limborch, _Theol. Christ._ I, c. 4; idem, _De veritate relig. Christ. collatio cum erudito Iudaeo_, 1687; Hugo Grotius, _De veritate relig. christ._, 1627; cf. Wijnmalen, _Hugo de Groot als verdediger van het Christ._, Utrecht, 1869. Through Descartes rationalism also penetrated the Reformed churches. _Theologia naturalis_ came to stand independently alongside _theologia revelata_. And within the latter, reason was granted the right to investigate and explain the credentials of revelation, H. A. Roëll, _Dissert. de theol. naturali_, 1700, and so forth. Leibniz expressed the generally prevailing opinion when he placed revelation over against reason as an extraordinary ambassador stands before a competent assembly. The assembly examines his credentials and, when it has found these genuine, listens to him reverently. _Discours sur la conformité de la foi avec la raison_, §29. Deism in England and rationalism in Germany soon inferred from this that _theologia naturalis_ was entirely sufficient. And supernaturalism, which conceded the emancipation of reason in _theologia naturalis_ and in the investigation of the truth of revelation, could appear before that reason with none but historical and rational proofs. Christianity was defended and dogmatics cultivated in this way by numerous men in England, Germany, and the Netherlands, of whom we recall here only the names of Butler, _The Analogy of Religion Natural and Revealed_, 1736; Paley, _View of the Evidences of Christianity_, 1794; idem, _Natural Theology_, 1802; Chalmers, _The Evidence and Authority of the Christ. Revelation_, 1817; idem, _Natural Theology_, 1823; cf. Tholuck, _Vermischte Schriften_ I, pp. 163–224; Reinhard, Morus, Doederlein, Knapp, Storr, and others; in this country Van Nuys Klinkenberg, Muntinghe, Heringa, Vinke, and others, and the works of the Hague Society. We further find this standpoint again later in Pareau and Hofstede de Groot, _Compendium Dogm. et Apolog. Christ._, 3 ed., 1848, p. 179 sq.; Van Oosterzee, who first adopted Schleiermacher’s standpoint, _Jaarb. v. wet. Theol._, 1845, pp. 1–74, but later sought help in an apologetics that preceded dogmatics, _Jaarb. v. wet. Theol._ V, p. 406; _De Leer der Herv. Kerk van J. H. Scholten beschouwd_, 1851, pp. 51, 53; _Dogmatiek_, §§30–34, §38, and so forth; Doedes, who wished to come to know Christianity by means of unprejudiced, purely historical investigation, _Het regt des Christ. tegenover de wijsbegeerte gehandhaafd_, 1847, _Modern of Apost. Christ._, 1860, _De zoogen. moderne Theol. eenigszins toegelicht_, 1862; abroad, Voigt, _Fundamentaldogmatik_, 1874, pp. 184 f., 232 f.; Gretillat, _Exposé de theol. systématique_ II 176 s.; A. B. Bruce, _Apologetics_, Edinburgh, Clark, 1892, p. 42; W. M. McPheeters, “Apostolical Sanction the Test of Canonicity,” _Presb. and Ref. Rev._, Jan. 1895; Ed. König, _Der Glaubensact des Christen_, Erlangen, 1891, p. 143 f.; and many other apologetic works; cf. Christlieb, art. “Apologetik” in Herzog².
