## 11.

Others have therefore defined religion in terms of moral action and sought its seat in the will. Pelagianism in its various forms—Semi-Pelagianism, Socinianism, Remonstrantism, deism, rationalism, and so forth—prepared this conception, insofar as in this tendency _fides_ is supplemented by, or even consists solely in, a _nova obedientia_. Doctrine is then only a means and is subordinate; love is the principal thing, and what matters is a virtuous, moral life. Alongside the intellectualistic conception, Spinoza also holds this moralistic conception. Holy Scripture is God’s word because it contains the _vera religio_, the _lex divina_, _Tract. theol. pol._ cap. 12 § 18 sq. It intends nothing _praeter obedientiam_, cap. 13, and this _obedientia erga Deum_ consists _in solo amore proximi_, ib. § 8. Philosophy and faith are so distinct that the former has _veritas_ as its goal, the latter _obedientia_ and _pietas_, cap. 14 § 38. The _intellectualis sive accurata Dei cognitio_ is not a gift to all believers, but _obedientia_ is, and this is required of all, cap. 13 § 9. But Kant above all gained acceptance for this moralism. Theoretical reason cannot reach the supersensible. God, freedom, and immortality cannot be demonstrated scientifically. They are only postulates of practical reason for the fulfillment of the moral law and the attainment of the highest good, that is, happiness joined with virtue. Believing is therefore holding something true not on theoretical but on practical grounds. Morality becomes the foundation of religion. And _Religion ist subjektiv betrachtet das Erkenntniss aller unserer Pflichten als göttlicher Gebote._ Religion here is not grounded directly and immediately in human nature, but only through the moral. Nor does it have any content and matter of its own, but it is nothing other than a further determination of the moral; the difference between the two lies not in the object but only in the form. Kant therefore had to acknowledge that no religion has contented itself with his religion. Besides the pure faith of reason, all religions contain many other _dogmata_, a _doktrinalen historischen Glauben_. But he explains this from the weakness of human nature, which is not easily convinced that a moral walk is all that God requires of us. The calling is to purify the _Kirchenglauben_ more and more and cause it to pass over into the pure _Vernunftglauben_, _Religion innerhalb der Grenzen der blossen Vernunft_, herausg. by K. Rosenkranz 1838. Kant’s religion therefore consisted in fact of nothing other than the rationalistic trilogy of God, virtue, and immortality. But J. G. Fichte went further and derived no other postulate from moral consciousness than that the Ego should regard the entire non-Ego, the world, as the “_versinnlichte Material_” of its duty and recognize it as so ordered that its moral willing and acting lie in the line of the moral goal of the whole—in other words, that there is a moral world order. Here religion is absorbed entirely into the moral. Cf. _Ueber den Grund unseres Glaubens an eine göttliche Weltregierung_ 1798. This moralism is deepened into ethicism by conceiving the moral not only psychologically but also metaphysically. Will, the good, and love are then true being, _Ursein_, the Absolute; from this everything—the Trinity, the world, redemption—is to be explained; this power of the good realizes itself in the human world by means of the moral. This is the fundamental thought from which Schelling proceeded in his second period: _es gibt in der letzten und höchsten Instanz gar kein anderes Sein als Wollen, Wollen ist Ursein_, _Ueber das Wesen der menschl. Freiheit_, _Sammtl. Werke_ VII 331f. Rothe in particular brought this ethicism into theology; _Frömmigkeit_ has no content of its own but coincides _in concreto_ with true morality, _Theol. Ethik_ § 114-126; the church therefore also passes over into the state, § 440. Since then this ethical tendency has gained great influence. The ethical is regarded in turn as the foundation (Neo-Kantians), the content (the _Ethischen_ among the _Vermittelungstheologen_ and among the Modernists), or even the complete substitute (_Morale indépendante_; the ethical movement in religion) for religion. The ethical tendency among the _Vermittelungstheologen_ followed in the footsteps of Schleiermacher and Rothe; cf. my _Theol. van D. Ch. de la Saussaye_, Leiden 1884, p. 20f. In the Netherlands Hoekstra sought the foundation of religious faith in practical reason, in the will, _Bronnen en grondslagen van ’t godsd. geloof_ 1864, pp. 23f., 49f., 63f. The ethical Modernists who followed him define religion as devotion to the moral ideal, as pure morality, Dr. Hooĳkaas, _God in de geschiedenis_ 1870. _Godsdienst volgens de beginselen der ethische richting onder de Modernen_, four lectures by Hooĳkaas, Hooĳkaas Herderschee, Oort, and van Hamel, ’s Bosch 1876. Cf. Rauwenhoff, _Wĳsb. v. d. godsd._ 1887 p. 116f. Rauwenhoff himself places the essence of religion in belief in a moral world order, _Wĳsb. v. d. godsd._ 373f. Related to this is the doctrine of the _Morale Indépendante_; cf. C. Coignet, _La morale indépendante dans son principe et dans son objet_, Paris 1863. Vacherot, _La religion_, Paris 1869; cf. _Bibl. v. mod. Theol._ 1870 I 333f. Caro, _La morale indép._, Paris 1876. E. Bersier, _sur la question de la mor. indép._, in _Handelingen der Ev. Alliantie_ 1867. Cramer, _Christendom en Humaniteit_ 1871 p. 159f. Likewise related is that ethical movement which penetrated from America into England and Germany and which, indifferent to every religion, recognizes no dogma but the love of the good. See W. M. Salter, _De godsdienst der moraal_, Dutch trans. by Dr. Hugenholtz 1889; cf. Lamers, _De godsd. evenmin moraal als metaphysica_ 1885. _Bibl. v. mod. Theol._ 1887, issue 4, p. 579. Kuenen in _de Hervorming_ 1888; Salter’s reply, ib., 15 Dec. 1888. Dr. Felix Adler, _Die ethischen Gezellschaften_, Berlin, Dümmler 1892. _Die ethische Bewegung in Deutschland, vorbereitende Mittheilungen eines Kreises gleichgesinnter Männer u. Frauen zu Berlin_, ib. 1892. St. Coit, _Die ethische Bewegung in der Religion_, vom Verf. durchges. Uebersetzung v. Gizycki. Leipzig, Reisland 1890. Against this: Dr. M. Keibel, _Die Religion u. ihr Recht gegenüber dem modernen Moralismus_, Halle, Pfeffer 1892. Also Otto Dreyer, _Undogmatisches Christenthum_, Braunschweig 1888; Egidy, _Ernste Gedanken_, Leipzig 1890 (cf. _Gids_, May 1893); H. Drummond, _Summum Bonum_, _Pax Vobiscum_, _The spiritual law in the natural world_; Tolstoy, _Vernunft u. Dogma_, _Wie ist mein Leben?_, both from Otto Janke, Berlin; _Worin besteht mein Glaube_, Leipzig 1885, and others, champion an undogmatic, practical, moral Christianity, a Christianity of the Sermon on the Mount.
