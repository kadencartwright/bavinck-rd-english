## 9.

The philosophy of religion has advanced especially three conceptions of the essence of _religio_ (_subjectiva_). First is the intellectualistic view, which locates the essence of religion in knowledge and places its seat in the understanding. Gnosticism already said that _gnosis_ saves, that _agnitio_ was _redemptio interioris hominis_, Irenaeus, _Adv. haer._ I, cap. 21. This Gnosticism has found defenders in the Christian church at all times, but it has reemerged especially in modern philosophy.

Spinoza regards understanding and will as _unum et idem_, _Eth._ II, prop. 49; causes the _amor Dei_ to be born from man’s clear and distinct knowledge of himself and his affections, ibid. V, prop. 15; and calls this love _intellectualis_, ibid. prop. 32. _Summa mentis virtus est Deum cognoscere_, V, prop. 27, and our _mens_ is _pars infiniti Dei intellectus_, II, prop. 11. This knowledge of God—that is, of things _sub specie aeternitatis_, II, prop. 44—is _summa mentis acquiescentia_, V, prop. 27.

According to Schelling in his first period, only an absolute knowledge of the Absolute, as the identity of the finite and infinite, is possible; religion therefore loses its independence entirely here, and faith is an incorrect, impure conception of the idea, _Philosophie u. Religion_ (_Sämmtl. Werke_, Erste Abth. VI, S. 11 f.). Hegel in particular elaborated this intellectualistic determination of the essence of religion. For him the Absolute is thought itself, which enters into oppositions and from them returns again to identity with itself. The whole world is therefore a development of spirit, a logical unfolding of the content of reason, a process in which the idea first objectifies itself in nature and then returns from nature to itself in spirit. Religion is one of the moments through which this process passes. It is the human spirit in whom the Absolute comes to itself and becomes conscious of itself. And this self-consciousness of the absolute spirit in the finite spirit is religion.

Religion is thus essentially knowledge, neither feeling nor action, but knowledge—indeed, knowledge of God by the finite spirit, or objectively God’s knowledge of himself through and in the finite spirit. _Der Mensch weiss nur von Gott, insofern Gott im Menschen von sich selbst weiss, diess Wissen ist Selbstbewustseyn Gottes, aber ebenso ein Wissen desselben vom Menschen, und diess Wissen Gottes vom Menschen ist Wissen des Menschen von Gott. Der Geist des Menschen von Gott zu wissen, ist nur der Geist Gottes selbst_, _Vorles. über die Philos. der Religion_, herausg. v. Marheinecke, 1832, _Werke_ XII, S. 428.

Religion, however, is not the highest knowledge; it is only a knowledge of the absolute in the form of sensible, historical representations. The highest, true knowledge is attained only in philosophy. Religion is therefore temporary, a lower form suited to the undeveloped. But philosophy releases the idea from the sensible representations of religion and thus arrives at an absolute, adequate, _begrifflich_ knowledge of God, ibid. S. 15 f.; cf. Strauss, _Christl. Gl._ I 12. Feuerbach, _Wesen des Christenthums_, 1841, and Strauss, _Christliche Glaubenslehre_, 1840, _Der alte und der neue Glaube_, 2e Aufl., 1872, drew the consequence and completed the breach with the Christian religion. Idealism turned into materialism.

Just as in the previous century with La Mettrie, cf. Stöckl, _Gesch. der neueren Philos._ I 378, so now once again many regarded belief in God as the greatest and most harmful error. Bruno Bauer, Arnold Ruge, Edgar Bauer, and Max Stirner preached egoism in its starkest form. The materialism that arose in Germany around 1850 judged no differently: Büchner, _Kraft und Stoff_, 1855, 16e Aufl., 1885, S. 392 f.; Specht, _Theol. u. Naturwiss._, 3e Aufl., 1878, S. 71 f.; Moleschott, _Kreislauf des Lebens_, 1852; Carl Vogt, _Köhlerglaube und Wissenschaft_, 1854; Czolbe, _Neue Darstellung des Sensualismus_, 1855.
