As in science, then, three kinds of _principia_ are also to be distinguished in religion. Religion exists because God is God and wishes to be served as God by his rational creatures. To that end he reveals himself to man in word and deed—the _principium cognoscendi externum_—and makes him subjectively capable of knowing and loving God through that revelation—the _principium cognoscendi internum_. The forms in which revelation occurs externally and internally can be modified in accordance with the different condition in which man as _creatura mutabilis_ may find himself. They differ in the _status integritatis_ and _corruptionis_, in the _status gratiae_ and _gloriae_. But the three _principia_ remain the same. There is no religion except because God makes himself known to man objectively and subjectively.

And once again these _principia_ in religion too find their foundation in the trinitarian being of God. It is the Father who reveals himself in the Son and through the Spirit.
