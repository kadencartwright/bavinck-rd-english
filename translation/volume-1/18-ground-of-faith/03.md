## 3.

The Reformation consciously and freely took its standpoint in the religious subject, in the faith of the Christian, in the testimony of the Holy Spirit.
To be sure, only a few statements concerning the _testimonium Spiritus Sancti_ occur in Luther, Zwingli, and Melanchthon, Köstlin, _Luthers Theol._ I 279, II 254 f.; Scholten, _L. H. K._ I 186 f.
But Calvin developed this doctrine at length and connected it not only with the content but also with the form and authority of Scripture.
That Scripture is God’s Word, Calvin says, is not established by the church but was established before its decision, for the church is founded upon the foundation of apostles and prophets.
Scripture brings its own authority with it, rests in itself, and is _αὐτοπιστος_.
Just as light is distinguished from darkness, white from black, and sweet from bitter, so Scripture is recognized by its own truth.
But Scripture obtains certainty among us as God’s Word only through the testimony of the Holy Spirit.
Proofs and reasonings are indeed of great value, but this testimony far surpasses them all in value; it is _omni ratione praestantius_.
Just as God alone can testify concerning himself in his Word, so his Word finds no belief in the human heart until it is sealed by the inward testimony of the Spirit.
The same Spirit who spoke through the mouth of the prophets must work in our hearts and convince us that they faithfully handed down what had been commanded to them by God.
The Holy Spirit is therefore the seal and confirmation of the faith of the devout.
If we have that testimony within us, we do not rest in any human judgment but establish beyond doubt, as though we beheld God himself in Scripture, that Scripture proceeded from God’s mouth through the ministry of human beings.
We submit to it _ut rei extra aestimandi aleam positae_.
But this must not be understood as though we blindly submitted to a matter unknown to us.
No, we are conscious that in that Scripture we possess invincible truth and feel _non dubiam vim numinis illic, vigere ac spirare_, by which, knowingly and willingly yet vitally and powerfully, we are compelled to obedience, _Inst._ I c. 7; _Comm. in 2 Tim._ 3:16.
In this doctrine of the _testimonium Sp. S._ Calvin understood himself to be describing not a private revelation but the experience of all believers, _Inst._ I, 7, 5.
For him this testimony of the Holy Spirit was also not isolated from the Spirit’s entire activity in the hearts of believers but stood in the closest connection with it; through this activity alone the whole church arises and exists; the entire application of salvation is a work of the Holy Spirit; and testimony concerning Scripture is only one of the Spirit’s many activities in the congregation of believers.
The _testimonium Sp. S._ also gives no new revelations but makes the believer firm concerning the truth of God that is contained fully in Scripture; it makes faith a _cognitio certa_ and excludes all doubt.
Finally, it finds its analogy in the testimony our conscience gives to the law of God and in the certainty we possess concerning God’s existence.
Cf. Klaiber, _Die Lehre der altprot. Dogm. von dem test. Sp. S._, _Jahrb. f. deutsche Theol._ 1857, S. 1-54; Aug. Benezeh, _Théorie de Calvin sur l’Ecriture Sainte_, Paris, 1890; Jacq. Pannier, _Le témoignage du Saint-Esprit_, Paris, 1893.
This doctrine of the _testim. Sp. S._ was received into the French, Dutch, and Westminster Confessions and was developed in Calvin’s spirit by Ursinus, _Tract. Theol._, p. 12, 13; Zanchi, _Op._ VIII col. 332 sq.; Polanus, _Synt. Theol._ I cap. 16; Trigland, _Antapologia_, p. 42 sq.; Maccovius, _Loci Comm._, p. 28; Alsted, _Theol. schol. didact._, 1618, p. 10 sq., 29 sq.; Maresius, _Syst. Theol._ I § 33, and others.
Outside Reformed theology too it gained acceptance among the Lutherans—not yet in Chemnitz, Heerbrand, and others, but certainly in Hutter, Hunnius, Gerhard, _Loc. Theol._, loc. 1 cap. 3 § 39; Quenstedt; and Hollaz; cf. Hase, _Hutterus Rediv._ § 37, 45; Schmid, _Dogm. der ev. luth. K._, 6e Aufl., S. 31; Klaiber, _Jahrb. f. d. Theol._ 1857, S. 1-54; Dorner, _Gesch. der Prot. Theol._, S. 539-549.

But this doctrine met opposition from Socinianism, Fock, _der Socinianismus_, 336 f.; from Remonstrantism, Episcopius, _Instit. Theol._, lib. 4, sect. 1, cap. 5, § 2; Limborch, _Theol. Christ._ I c. 4 § 17; and from Romanism, Bellarmine, _de verbo Dei_ IV 4; cf. Pannier, _Le témoignage du S. Esprit_, p. 140 s.
Gradually the _testimonium internum_ began to lose its place of honor in Reformed theology as well.
In Turretin, _Theol. El._ II qu. 6 § 11 sq. and _Decas disput. miscell._, p. 30-70; Amyraldus, _Syntagma thesium theol. in acad. Salmur._, 1665, p. 117-143; Molinaeus, _Juge des controverses_, chap. 16, 17; and others, it is already weakened and identified with such an illumination of the Holy Spirit as enables the understanding to notice the _notae_ and criteria of the divinity of Holy Scripture.
Faith no longer attaches itself directly and immediately to Scripture but flows from insight into the _veritatis et divinitatis notae_.
Between Scripture and faith, the marks of Scripture’s truth are interposed.
At first this was still done in the sense that noticing and discerning those criteria was ascribed to an illumination of the understanding by the Holy Spirit.
But rationalism soon considered even this unnecessary, entrusted the investigation of the truth of revelation to reason, and grounded the authority of Scripture upon historical proofs.
Even orthodox theologians hardly dared any longer to speak of the _testimonium internum_, Brakel, _Red. godsd._ 2:7; Marck, _Merch der Godg._ 2:6.
If the testimony of the Holy Spirit was still introduced in confirmation of the authority of Scripture, it came entirely at the end and was transformed into an argument from experience, Reinhard, _Dogm._, 5te Aufl., S. 69; Bretschneider, _Dogm._, 1838, I 283, and the literature cited there; Hase, _Hutterus Rediv._ § 37, note 4; Vinke, _Theol. Christ. dogm. Comp._, p. 21, 22, and so forth.
Michaelis, _Dogm._, 2e Aufl., Gött., 1784, S. 92, declared that he had never perceived such a testimony of the Holy Spirit in his heart and did not find it in Scripture either.
And Strauss, _Gl._ I 136, claimed that the _testimonium Sp. S._ led to fanaticism or to rationalism and was the Achilles’ heel of the Protestant system.

Various causes, however, contributed to restoring this doctrine of the _testimonium internum_ somewhat to honor.
Kant’s critique of rationalism; the _Beweis des Geistes und der Kraft_ to which Lessing appealed; the Romanticism of Jacobi and Schleiermacher; and the barrenness of apologetics gave rise to the conviction that the justification of the Christian religion must proceed from the faith of the congregation.
Religious truth must be proved in a different manner from a proposition in _mathesis_.
Revelation, which is the _principium externum_ of religion, must be answered by a corresponding organ within the human being himself.
All this admittedly still differs greatly from the _testimonium Sp. S._ as Calvin developed it.
Modern theologians often strip this testimony of its entire supernatural character.
Usually they connect it not with the form but only with the content, and sometimes only with the religious-ethical content of Scripture.
But Protestant theologians, like Roman Catholic theologians, must acknowledge that the final and deepest ground of faith cannot lie outside us in proofs and reasonings, in church and tradition, but can be found only within the human being himself, in the religious subject.
And that conviction works to the advantage of the doctrine of the _testimonium Sp. S._
An occasional theologian may still defend the historical proofs and oppose the _testimonium Sp. S._, as does König, _Der Glaubensakt des Christen_, 1891, S. 92-99; id., _Die letzte Instanz des bibl. Glaubens_, 1892, S. 23 f.
Most theologians again include it in dogmatics and assign it a larger or smaller place, Oosterzee, _Jaarb. v. wet. Theol._ 1845; _Dogm._ I 242; Scholten, _L. H. K._ I 115-233; Twesten I 433-7; Klaiber, _Jahrb. f. d. Theol._ 1857; Lange I § 84; Kahnis, _Luth. Dogm._ I 291; Philippi, _Kirchl. Dogm._, 3e Aufl., I 135 f.; Cremer in Herzog² 6, 746; Frank, _Syst. der chr. Gewissheit_ I 139; id., _Dogm. Studiën_, 38-57; Thomasius, _Christi Person u. Werk_, 3e Aufl., II 268 f.; Dorner, _Gesammelte Schriften_, Berlin, 1883, S. 131 f.; Lipsius, _Jahrb. f. prot. Theol._ 1885, S. 614; Hodge, _Syst. Theol._ III 69; Pannier, _Le témoignage du S. Esprit_, p. 193 s.; Kuyper, _Encycl._ II 501-511; John de Witt, _The testimony of the Holy Spirit to the Bible_, _Presb. and Ref. Rev._, Jan. 1895, p. 69-85.
