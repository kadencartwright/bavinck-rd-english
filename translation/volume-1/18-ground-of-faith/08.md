## 8.

This testimony of the Holy Spirit is not nullified by the fact that it appears so different in believers. Bellarmine already objected that, despite this _testimonium Sp. S._, Luther and Calvin rendered very different judgments concerning the epistle of James. But in the hearts of believers the Holy Spirit bears witness not only concerning Holy Scripture but equally with respect to all the other truths of salvation. There is no church that does not in this sense accept a testimony, an illumination, of the Holy Spirit. Yet this does not exclude difference in the confession of the various truths. We believe one holy catholic Christian church, a communion of saints; all believers confess one Lord, one faith, one baptism, and have been made to drink of one Spirit; yet there is division and conflict among the churches and concerning the principal articles of faith. The unity of the Christian church with respect to Scripture is far greater than with respect to any other dogma, not even excepting that of the Trinity and the deity of Christ. Yet all this difference does not cause us to doubt the unity of faith and knowledge, the catholicity of the church, or the guidance of the Spirit into all truth; for difference will remain as long as the church is imperfect, the heart corrupted, insight limited, and faith small and weak. In the individual believer the _testimonium Spiritus Sancti_ is not always equally strong and loud; because it is most closely connected with faith and the life of faith, it rises and falls and is subject to doubt and assault. When sin gains the upper hand in the believer, the consciousness of his forgiveness is darkened and the testimony of the Holy Spirit loses force. Our faith in Scripture rises and falls with our trust in Christ. The confession of the _testimonium Spiritus Sancti_ is so high and ideal that the reality of Christian life often remains far beneath it.

In addition, Scripture is certainly a book also for the individual believer, but nevertheless in connection with the church of all ages. Scripture was given to the whole church, to believers of all times and places. The individual believer always nourishes himself with a small portion of Scripture. There are entire portions of Scripture that remain a closed book for individual believers, indeed for entire churches and ages. But the confession of Scripture as God’s Word is a confession of the whole church with which the individual believer agrees and which, for his own part and according to the measure of his faith, he also supports and maintains. Hofmann, _Weiss. und Erf._ I 45 f. The _testimonium Spiritus Sancti_ is not a private opinion but the testimony of the church of all ages, of the whole of Christendom, of the whole regenerate humanity. That church too once stood in all its members, just like the world, hostile over against the Word of God. But in and with her the Holy Spirit took up the cause of Christ’s truth. He broke her enmity, enlightened her understanding, bent her will, and preserves her in the truth from age to age and from day to day. The entire confession of the congregation is a _testimonium Spiritus Sancti_. It is the yes and amen that the congregation pronounces upon the truth of God. It is the “Abba, Father; your Word is the truth” that rises from the hearts of all believers. So little is the _testimonium Spiritus Sancti_ the Achilles’ heel of Protestantism that it deserves rather to be called the cornerstone of the Christian confession, the crown and seal of all Christian truth, the triumph of the Holy Spirit in the world. Take away the testimony of the Holy Spirit, not only in relation to Scripture but to all truths of salvation, and there is no church left. For the testimony the Holy Spirit gives to Scripture as the Word of God is only a single note in the song he places upon the lips of the congregation; it is only a small part of that great, divine work entrusted to the Holy Spirit, namely to cause the fullness of Christ to dwell in his congregation. But conceived in this way, this testimony of the Holy Spirit is also not devoid of all value over against opponents. When it is detached from its whole setting and cut off from the life of faith in which it is rooted, from the communion of saints in which it blossoms, and from the whole of Christian truth with which it is connected, then certainly it loses all its force over against opponents, and the yes of the one is no stronger than the no of the other. But conceived as the testimony borne by the Holy Spirit in the hearts of all God’s children concerning the truth that is in Christ the Lord, it does not fail to make an impression upon the heart, even of the most obstinate opponent. Even then it does not come to stand on one level with a logical argument or mathematical proof. It retains a power of its own. But science would be in an unhappy state if it could take account only of what is demonstrable. Does conscience have no power because the immoral person at every moment goes against its testimony? Do the _principia_ of science deserve no faith because the skeptic refuses to acknowledge them? Is Holy Scripture powerless because its truth cannot be demonstrated to the psychical man? The power of all these moral realities lies precisely in the fact that they do not demonstrate themselves but place themselves in high majesty before everyone’s consciousness. They are powerful through the authority with which they appear. A father does not prove his authority to his children but maintains it with _droit divin_. And so does Holy Scripture. It has maintained its authority in the congregation of Christ to this day; it has caused all believers, and among them the greatest minds and noblest souls, to bow before its authority. What power in the world can be compared with that of Scripture? The _testimonium Spiritus Sancti_ is the victory of the foolishness of the cross over the wisdom of the world, the triumph of the thoughts of God over the reasonings of man. In this sense the testimony of the Holy Spirit possesses an excellent apologetic value. For this is the victory that overcomes the world, namely our faith.
