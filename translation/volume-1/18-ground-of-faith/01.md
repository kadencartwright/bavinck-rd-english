# § 18. The Ground of Faith

## 1.

As soon as Christian theology began to reflect seriously on the ultimate and deepest ground of faith, it came to recognize that no intellectual or historical proof advanced for the truth of revelation could ultimately serve as that ground.
The apologists of the 2nd century attached great value to those proofs, above, p. 423, and emphasized over against pagan Naturalism that faith was a rational, free act of the human being: Justin Martyr, _Dial._ c. 88, 102, 131; Theophilus, _Ad Autol._ II 27; Irenaeus, _Adv. haer._ IV 37, 5, etc.
Yet even among them we already find the awareness that all these proofs are powerless actually to move anyone to faith.
For that something else and something more is needed, namely, divine grace: Justin Martyr, _Dial._ c. 119; _Apol._ 1, 10; 2, 10; Irenaeus, _Adv. haer._ IV 39, 2; Origen, _De princ._ I 10, 14, 19.
Irenaeus compares this grace to the dew and rain that make the field fruitful, ibid. III 17, 2, 3.
But Augustine was the first to see and confess clearly the necessity of _gratia interna_.
Elsewhere he ascribes great value to the church as a motive for faith, but his doctrine of _gratia interna_ proves that for him the church was not the ultimate and deepest cause of his faith.
That was God alone.
_Operatur Deus fidem nostram miro modo agens in cordibus nostris, ut credamus_, _De praed. sanct._ 2, 6.
After all, believing is always voluntary: _nemo credit nisi volens_.
And therefore through his grace God bends the will and causes us to believe with the understanding, _Conf._ 13, 1; _De div. quaest._ 1, qu. 2, n. 21; _De praed. sanct._ 19; _De dono persev._ 16, etc.
Later theologians too ascribed great power to the _praeambula fidei_ and the _motiva credibilitatis_; and among these the church gradually occupied an ever more prominent place.
But all admit that these _motiva_ make revelation _non evidenter veram_ but only _evidenter credibilem_.
They exclude reasonable doubt and show that it is not unreasonable to believe but is indeed unreasonable to assume the contrary, Thomas, _S. Theol._ II 2, qu. 1, art. 5 ad 2; _S. c. Gent._ I, c. 9.
But they are not conclusive.
Indeed, when treating the philosophical proofs for the Trinity, Thomas expressly says: _qui autem probare nititur trinitatem personarum naturali ratione, fidei derogat_, _S. Theol._ I, qu. 32, art. 1.
Against opponents proofs are of little avail, _quia ipsa rationum insufficientia eos magis in suo errore confirmaret, dum aestimarent nos propter tam debiles rationes veritati fidei consentire_, _S. c. Gent._ I, c. 9.
However credible revelation may be made by the proofs, it is and remains a truth of faith, Bellarmine, _de Conc. et Eccl._ IV 3.
At Rome it must remain so for this reason as well: otherwise the voluntariness and thereby the meritoriousness of faith would be lost, Thomas, _S. Theol._ II 2, qu. 2, art. 9 and 10.
Faith therefore accepts the truth not on the ground of its own insight but on that of divine authority.
_Non enim fides assentit alicui, nisi quia est a Deo revelatum_, Thomas, _S. Theol._ II 2, qu. 1, art. 1; _S. c. Gent._ I, c. 9; Becanus, II 2, p. 3–17; Billuart, II 2, Tom. 1, _de fide_, p. 1 sq.; Dens, II 280 sq.; Jansen, I 701–711.
And for that authority of God to be acknowledged, a change of the will must first take place in the human being.
Believing is indeed an act of the understanding, Thomas, _S. Theol._ II 2, qu. 4, art. 2, but it presupposes a bending of the will through grace.
The understanding must be determined to faith by the will, _S. Theol._ II 2, qu. 2, art. 1 ad 3.
The _assensus fidei_ is therefore solely _a Deo, interius movente per gratiam_, ibid. I, qu. 62, art. 2 ad 3; II 1, qu. 109, art. 6; qu. 112, art. 2; II 2, qu. 6, art. 1.
In the brief of 26 September 1835, Gregory XVI condemned the view of Hermes that the _motiva credibilitatis_ were the proper ground of faith in revelation.
And the Vatican Council established in sess. 3, _Const. dogm. de fide_, cap. 3, that although the motives could make divine revelation _credibilis_, _nemo tamen evangelicae praedicationi consentire potest, sicut oportet ad salutem consequendam absque illuminatione et inspiratione Spiritus Sancti_.
