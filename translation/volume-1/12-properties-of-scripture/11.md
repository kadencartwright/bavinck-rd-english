## 11.

Scripture is the only adequate means of preserving the spoken Word uncorrupted and making it the possession of all human beings. _Vox audita perit, littera scripta manet._ The brevity of life, the unfaithfulness of memory, the deceitfulness of the heart, and all manner of other dangers threatening the purity of transmission make the recording of the spoken word absolutely necessary for its preservation and dissemination. This applies to the Word of revelation in a still higher degree. For the gospel is not according to man; it stands directly opposed to his thoughts and wishes, as divine truth over against his falsehood.

Moreover, revelation is intended not for one generation and one age but for all peoples and ages. It must complete its course through the whole of humanity and to the end of the ages. Truth is one; Christianity is a _Universalreligion_. How can this destiny of the Word of revelation be attained except by its being recorded and written down?

The church cannot perform this service of the Word. Nowhere is infallibility promised to it. In Scripture it is always referred to the objective Word, to the law and the testimony. Properly speaking, even Rome itself does not claim this. In Rome the church—that is, the assembly of believers—is not infallible, nor is the assembly of bishops, but only the pope. The declaration of papal infallibility is proof of the Reformation proposition concerning the unreliability of tradition, the fallibility of the church, and even the necessity of Scripture. For this declaration of infallibility entails that the truth of the Word of revelation is not and cannot be preserved by the church as the assembly of believers, since it too remains subject to error, but can be explained only from a special assistance of the Holy Spirit, in which, according to the Roman claim, the pope shares.

Rome and the Reformation therefore agree that the Word of revelation in and for the church can remain purely preserved only through the institution of the apostolate—that is, through inspiration. The dispute concerns only whether that apostolate has ceased or is continued in the pope.

By contrast, the claim of mediation theology that Scripture proceeded from the church and that the church is therefore properly the _Verfasserin der Bibel_ is wholly untenable. This can be claimed only by misconceiving the proper office of prophets and apostles, identifying inspiration with regeneration, and detaching Scripture entirely from revelation. According to the doctrine of Scripture, however, inspiration is a special act of the Holy Spirit, a special gift to prophets and apostles, by which they could hand down the Word of God purely and uncorrupted to the church of all ages. Scripture therefore did not proceed from the church but was given to the church through a special activity of the Holy Spirit in prophets and apostles. Scripture belongs to the revelation bestowed by God upon his people. Rome and the Reformation are agreed on this.

But over against Rome the Reformation maintains that this special activity of the Holy Spirit has now ceased—in other words, that the apostolate no longer exists and is not continued in the pope. The apostles deposited their testimony concerning Christ completely and purely in Holy Scripture. Through it they made the revelation of God the possession of humanity. Scripture is the Word of God that has entered fully into the world. It makes that Word universal and eternal, wrests it from error and falsehood, forgetfulness and transience.

As humanity becomes larger, life shorter, memory weaker, science more extensive, error more serious, and falsehood bolder, the _necessitas S. Scripturae_ increases. In every field writing and the press gain in significance. The printing press was a giant step toward heaven and hell. Holy Scripture too shares in this course of development. Its necessity comes ever more clearly to light. It is disseminated and made common property as never before. It is translated into hundreds of languages. It comes before everyone’s eyes and into everyone’s hands. Increasingly it proves to be the suitable means of bringing truth to the knowledge of all human beings.

That religious literature alongside it remains for many the principal nourishment of their spiritual life proves nothing against the necessity of Holy Scripture. For all Christian truth is nevertheless drawn from it directly or indirectly. The derivative stream too receives its water from the source. It is untenable to claim that anything of Christian truth would now still come to us outside and apart from Holy Scripture. In the first century such a thing was possible, but now the streams of tradition and Scripture have long since flowed together, and the former has long since been taken up into the latter.

Rome can maintain this only through its doctrine of the continuation of the apostolate and the infallibility of the pope. But for a Protestant this is impossible. The Christian character of truth can be demonstrated solely by the fact that it is rooted with all its fibers in Holy Scripture. There is no knowledge of Christ except from Scripture, no communion with him except through communion with the Word of the apostles. Cf.
