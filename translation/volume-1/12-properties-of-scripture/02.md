## 2.

From this Roman Catholic conception of the relation between Scripture and church flow all the differences that exist between Rome and the Reformation in the doctrine of Scripture. They concern especially the necessity of Holy Scripture, the Apocrypha of the Old Testament, the _editio Vulgata_, the prohibition of the Bible, the interpretation of Scripture, and tradition. Formally, the reversal in the relation between Scripture and church appears most clearly in the fact that modern Roman Catholic theologians treat the doctrine of the church in the _pars formalis_ of dogmatics. The church belongs to the _principia fidei_. As Scripture is for the Reformation, so the church, the _magisterium_, or properly the pope, is the formal principle, the _fundamentum fidei_, in Romanism, Jansen, I 829.

Over against this the Reformers placed the doctrine of the properties of Scripture. It bore an entirely polemical character, but for that very reason was also established in its main features from the beginning, Heppe, _Dogm. d. deutschen Prot._ I 207–257. Gradually it was also incorporated more or less systematically and methodically into dogmatics—not yet in Zwingli, Calvin, Melanchthon, and others, but already in Musculus, _Loci Comm._, 1567, pp. 374 sq.; Zanchi, _De S. Script._, _Op._ VIII 319 sq.; Polanus, _Synt. Theol._ 17 sq.; Junius, _Theses Theol._, _Op._ I 1594 sq., and others; and in the Lutheran church in Gerhard, Quenstedt, Calovius, Hollaz, and others.

But the treatment differed. Sometimes all manner of historical and critical material was discussed in it; dogmatics incorporated almost the whole “Introduction,” the _canonica generalis_ and _specialis_. The number and division of the properties were also stated differently. Authority, utility, necessity, truth, clarity, sufficiency, origin, division, content, Apocrypha, council, church, tradition, _editio authentica_, translations, interpretation, proofs, _testimonium Sp. S._—all this and much more was treated in the doctrine of Scripture and its properties.

Gradually the material was more narrowly delimited. Calovius and Quenstedt distinguished between _affectiones primariae_ and _secundariae_. To the first belonged _auctoritas, veritas, perfectio, perspicuitas, semet ipsam interpretandi facultas, judicialis potestas_ and _efficacia_; and among the latter were reckoned _necessitas, integritas, puritas, authentia_ and _legendi omnibus concessa licentia_. Still simpler was the order often followed: _auctoritas, necessitas, perfectio seu sufficientia, perspicuitas, semet ipsam interpretandi facultas_ and _efficacia_. Hase, _Hutterus Rediv._ § 43 f.; Schmid, _Dogm. der ev. luth. K._, S. 27 f.; Herzog² 2, 365 f.; Heppe, _Dogm. der ev. ref. K._ 9 f.; Voigt, _Fundamentaldogm._, S. 644 f.

But even here further simplification can be made. Historical, critical, archaeological, and other material does not belong in dogmatics but in the bibliological disciplines of theology. The _authentia, integritas, puritas_, and so forth, can therefore not be treated fully in dogmatics; they are discussed there only insofar as the doctrine of Scripture also supplies some data concerning their condition. After inspiration and authority, _veritas_ needs no separate argument and would thereby be weakened rather than strengthened. _Efficacia_ finds its place in the doctrine of the _media gratiae_. Thus only _auctoritas, necessitas, perfectio_ and _perspicuitas_ remain.

Among these there is still this difference: _auctoritas_ is not a property coordinated with the others, for it is given of itself with inspiration. _Necessitas, perspicuitas_ and _sufficientia_, by contrast, do not flow from inspiration in the same sense. It is conceivable that an infallible Scripture would have to be supplemented and interpreted by an infallible tradition. Rome indeed acknowledges the authority of Scripture but denies its other properties.
