## 2.

But this speculative method also found acceptance in the school of Schleiermacher. Schleiermacher shared the subjective starting point with Hegel, but took his position not in reason but in feeling. Religion is a peculiar determination of feeling or of immediate self-consciousness. Further, he conceived that feeling not individually but historically, as it existed in a religious community and as in the Christian congregation it had been formed particularly by Jesus as the _Erlöser_. Finally, he saw in dogmatics no speculative development but only a description of pious states of mind or of the faith of the congregation; in theory Schleiermacher opposed every mixing of theology and philosophy. On the other hand, Schleiermacher’s _Glaubenslehre_ nevertheless showed that his own religious experience diverged very sharply from that of the Christian congregation and had certainly been formed in part under the influence of Spinoza; and in his _Kurze Darstellung_ he gave first place to philosophical theology, which received the task of determining the essence of Christianity. In fact, theology in Schleiermacher became entirely dependent on philosophy, _Kurze Darst._ §§24, 32 f.; cf. _Christl. Glaub._ §2 f. With Schleiermacher, the _Vermittelungstheologie_ took its standpoint in consciousness, in faith, in the confession of the congregation, but joined to it Hegel’s speculative method in order thereby to elevate faith into knowledge and cause authority to yield to independent rational insight. Twesten strives by means of reasoning to derive an orthodox Lutheran dogmatics from pious feeling, _Vorles._ I² 21 f. Müller wishes, by a regressive path, through reflection on his own experience, to come from the religious certainty of faith to an objective knowledge of God, _Dogm. Abhandlungen_, 1870, pp. 1–42, and art. “Dogmatik” in Herzog¹. Martensen assigns to regenerated consciousness the task of scientifically reproducing from its own depths the doctrine of Scripture and the church, _Dogm._ §31 f. In Dorner the _Glaubenslehre_ is not merely descriptive but also constructive and progressive; it brings religious knowledge to systematic _Begründung und Entfaltung_ and tries to demonstrate the Christian idea of God as necessary by showing that it supplements and completes the concept of God as such, _Glaub._ I 155 f. This speculative method of the _Vermittelungstheologie_ emerges especially clearly in Rothe; the consciousness of God is the idea from which, with the aid of Hegelian dialectic, he derives the whole of creation, nature, history, sin, and redemption, _Theol. Ethik_ §7. There is, for that matter, great diversity among the _Vermittelungstheologen_, but they all share a common starting point and method: they proceed not from one authority or another but from the Christian consciousness of the congregation, and seek the proof for the truth of faith not in an appeal to some authority but in inner evidence, in the necessity of thought.
