Revelation according to Scripture is therefore a historical process, an organic system, a continual divine action for breaking the power of sin, establishing his kingdom, restoring the cosmos, and bringing about the ἀνακεφαλαιωσις των παντων ἐν Χριστῳ, Eph. 1:10. In theophany God once again sets himself as the only and true God over against the idols of man’s own devising. In prophecy he places his thought as truth over against the lie of Satan, and in miracle he demonstrates his divine power over against all works of unrighteousness. In revelation God posits and maintains his I over against every non-I and, despite all resistance, brings it to universal acknowledgment and complete triumph.

All revelation is therefore soteriological. It proceeds from Christ in both the Old and New Testaments. But “soteriological” must then be understood in the sense of Scripture, not in a religious-ethical sense, as though revelation contained only religious and ethical truth; still less in an intellectual sense, as though revelation consisted only in doctrine. Rather, it is soteriological in the scriptural sense—namely, that the content of revelation is not doctrine or life or an affection of the disposition, but is all these together: a divine work, a world of thoughts and deeds, an _ordo gratiae_ that combats and conquers the _ordo peccati_ in every sphere.

The goal of revelation is not only to teach man and enlighten his understanding (Rationalism), to cause him to practice virtue (Moralism), or to cultivate religious affections in him (Mysticism). God’s goal with his revelation extends much farther and more broadly. It is nothing other than to wrest man, humanity, and the world from the power of sin and once again cause the name of God to shine forth in every creature. Sin has corrupted and devastated everything: understanding and will, the ethical and the physical world. God’s concern in his revelation is therefore also the salvation and restoration of the whole man and the entire cosmos. God’s revelation is most certainly soteriological, but the object of this σωτηρια is the cosmos—not only the ethical or the will to the exclusion of the understanding, and not only the psychical to the exclusion of the somatic and physical, but all of them together. For God has shut up all under sin that he might have mercy upon all, Rom. 5:15 f.; 11:32; Gal. 3:22.
