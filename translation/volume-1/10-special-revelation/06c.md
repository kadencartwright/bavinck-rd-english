When the revelation of God in Christ, in his person and work, has appeared and been described in Scripture, another dispensation begins. The Holy Spirit came to dwell in the church; with this the character of the times was changed. The αἰων οὑτος passed over into the αἰων μελλων. Just as in the first period everything was prepared for Christ, so now everything is derived from him. Then Christ was formed as the head of the church; now the church is formed as the body of Christ. Then the Word, Holy Scripture, was completed; now it is worked out.

Yet this dispensation too occupies a place in the system of revelation. Revelation has continued, though modified according to the nature of the dispensation. Revelation as intending and producing the Christ has reached its end. For Christ is present, his work is accomplished, and his word is complete. No new constitutive elements can be added to the _revelatio specialis_. The question whether the gift of prediction and miracles still continues in the Christian church is therefore also of subordinate importance. The testimonies of the church fathers are so numerous and powerful that for the earliest times this question can scarcely be answered in the negative. Thomas, II 2, qu. 178; Voetius, _Disp._ II 1002 sq.; Gerhard, _Loci_, loc. 22, sectio 11; Dr. C. Middleton, _A free inquiry into the miraculous powers, which are supposed to have subsisted in the Christian Church_, 3 ed., London, 1749; Tholuck, _Ueber die Wunder der Kath. Kirche_, _Verm. Schriften_ I 28–148; J. H. Newman, _Two essays on scripture miracles and on ecclesiastical_, 2 ed., London, 1870; H. Müller, _Natur und Wunder_, Strassburg, 1892, S. 182.

But even if those gifts have remained, the content of the _revelatio specialis_, which is concentrated in Christ and deposited in Scripture, is not enriched by them. And if, according to Augustine’s thought, _De civ._ 22, 8; _De util. cred._ 16; _De vera relig._ 25, they diminished and ceased, that revelation is not impoverished by this. However this may be, the _revelatio specialis_ in Christ nevertheless continues in a certain sense in this dispensation. Even if all prediction in the Christian church has ceased and every miracle in the proper sense has passed away, the church itself is from moment to moment a product of revelation. Spiritual miracles continue. The grace of God in Christ glorifies itself in illumination and regeneration, in faith and conversion, in sanctification and preservation.

Christ is mediator; his concern is the church. He came to renew the world and recreate humanity according to the image of God. The _revelatio specialis_ finds its goal in bringing about a new order of things. And therefore it continues even now in a modified form—that is, in a spiritual sense. It finds its resting point only in the epiphany of Christ, in the new heaven and the new earth in which righteousness dwells.
