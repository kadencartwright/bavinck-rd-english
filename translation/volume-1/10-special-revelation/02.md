## 2. Theophany (Angelophany, Christophany)

Holy Scripture repeatedly speaks of an appearance of God, sometimes without any further description, Gen. 12:7; 17:1, 22; 26:2, 24; 35:9; Ex. 6:2; cf. also Gen. 11:5; Ex. 4:24; 12:12, 23; 17:6; Num. 23:4, 16; 1 Sam. 3:21; 2 Sam. 5:24; but elsewhere in a dream, Gen. 20:3; 28:12 f.; 31:24; 1 Kings 3:5; 9:2; or also in prophetic vision, 1 Kings 22:19 f.; Isa. 6; Ezek. 1:4 f.; 3:12 f.; 8:4 f.; 10:1 f.; 43:2 f.; 44:4; Amos 7:7; 9:1; Dan. 7:9 f.; Luke 2:9; 2 Pet. 1:17. Still more frequently he appears in clouds of smoke and fire as signs of his presence: thus to Abraham, Gen. 15:17 f.; to Moses, Ex. 3:2; 33:18 f.; on Sinai, Ex. 19:9, 16 f.; 24:16; cf. vv. 9–11; Deut. 5:23; 9:15; over the people, Ex. 13:21 f.; 14:19–24; 40:38; Num. 9:21; 14:14; Deut. 1:33; Neh. 9:12, 19; Ps. 78:14; above the tabernacle, Ex. 33:9; 40:34 f.; Lev. 9:23; Num. 9:15–23; 11:17, 25; 12:5; 17:7; 20:6; Deut. 31:15; Ps. 99:7; Isa. 4:5; and in the holy of holies, Ex. 25:8, 22; 29:45, 46; Lev. 16:2; 26:11, 12; Num. 7:89; cf. also to Elijah, 1 Kings 19:11 f.

These appearances presuppose no corporeality in God, Ex. 20:4; 33:20; Deut. 4:12, 15, but are sensibly perceptible signs by which his presence becomes known, just as the Holy Spirit makes himself known on the day of Pentecost through wind and fire. Nor should one think here of an emanation of this cloud from the divine being, but of a presence of God revealing itself in creaturely forms. In those signs the divine glory, כָּבוֹד, δοξα, becomes manifest, Ex. 16:20; 24:17; Lev. 9:6, 23, 24; Num. 14:10; 16:19; 20:6. That glory is therefore also described as a consuming fire, Ex. 24:7; Lev. 9:23, 24, and as a cloud, 1 Kings 8:10, 11; Isa. 6:4.

But God appears not only in impersonal signs; he also visits his people in personal beings. Surrounded and served by many thousands of angels, Isa. 6:2, 6, he sends them in human form to this earth to make his word and will known. They already occur in Gen. 18; 19; 28:12; 32:1, 2; Deut. 33:2; Job 33:23; 1 Kings 13:18, and according to Acts 7:53; Gal. 3:19, they also served at the giving of the law. But they are mediators of revelation especially after the exile, Dan. 8:13; 9:11; 10:5; Zech. 1:7–6:5. They appear still more frequently in the New Testament. They are present at the birth of Jesus, Matt. 1:20; 2:13, 19; Luke 1:11; 2:9; repeatedly in his life, John 1:52; Matt. 4:6; at his suffering, Matt. 26:53; Luke 22:43; at his resurrection and ascension, Matt. 28:2, 5; Luke 24:23; John 20:12; Acts 1:10. They occur repeatedly in the history of the apostles, Acts 5:19; 8:26; 10:3; 11:13; 12:7; 23:9; 27:23; Rev. 22:6, 16. And at his return Christ is accompanied by the angels, Matt. 16:27; 25:31; Mark 8:38; Luke 9:26; 1 Thess. 3:13, etc.

Among all these messengers of God, the מלאך יהוה occupies a special place. He appears to Hagar, Gen. 16:6–13; 21:17–20; to Abraham, Gen. 18; 19; 22; 24:7, 40; to Jacob, Gen. 28:13–17; 31:11–13; 32:24–30; cf. Hos. 12:4; Gen. 48:15, 16; to and in the time of Moses, Ex. 3:2 f.; 13:21; 14:19; 23:20–23; 32:34; 33:2 f.; cf. Num. 20:16; Isa. 63:8, 9; and further in Josh. 5:13, 14; Judg. 6:11–24; 13:2–23. This _Malak YHWH_ is neither an insubstantial symbol nor a created angel, but a personal, adequate revelation and appearance of God, distinguished from him, Ex. 23:20–23; 33:14 f.; Isa. 63:8, 9, and yet one with him in name, Gen. 16:13; 31:13; 32:28, 30; 48:15, 16; Ex. 3:2 f.; 23:21; Judg. 13:1, 2; in power, Gen. 16:10, 11; 21:18; 18:14, 18; Ex. 14:21; Judg. 6:21; in deliverance and blessing, Gen. 48:16; Ex. 3:8; 23:20; Isa. 63:8, 9; and in worship and honor, Gen. 18:3; 22:12; Ex. 23:21.

After the deliverance from Egypt the _Malak YHWH_ recedes. God dwells among his people in the temple, 1 Kings 8:10 f.; 2 Chron. 7:1 f.; Ps. 68:17; 74:2; 132:13 f.; 135:21. To that place the longing of the souls of Israel’s pious goes out, Ps. 27:4; 42; 43; 48; 50; 63:3; 65; 84; 122; 137. But this theophany is imperfect. God does not dwell in a house made with hands, 1 Kings 8:27; Jer. 7:4; Mic. 3:11; Acts 7:48; 17:24. Only the high priest might enter the holy of holies once a year. In the Old Testament theophany does not yet attain its end and goal. Another and more glorious coming of God to his people is therefore still expected, both for deliverance and for judgment, Ps. 50:3; 96:13; Isa. 2:21; 30:27; 40 f., passim; Mic. 1:3; 4:7; Zeph. 3:8; Joel 3:17; Zech. 2:10 f.; 14:9. The Angel of the covenant appears once again in prophecy, Zech. 1:8–12; 3, and will come to his temple, Mal. 3:1.

Theophany reaches its climax in Christ, who is the ἀγγελος, δοξα, εἰκων, λογος, υἱος του θεου, in whom God is fully revealed and fully given, Matt. 11:27; John 1:14; 14:9; Col. 1:15; 2:19, etc. Through him and the Spirit whom he sends, God’s dwelling among and within his people already now becomes true, spiritual reality, John 14:23; Rom. 8:9, 11; 2 Cor. 6:16. The church is the house of God, the temple of the Holy Spirit, Matt. 18:20; 1 Cor. 3:16; 6:19; Eph. 2:21. But this indwelling of God in Christ’s church is not yet the last and highest. It reaches its full realization only in the new Jerusalem. Then the tabernacle of God is with human beings, and he will dwell with them, and they will be his people, and God himself will be with them and be their God. They will see his face, and his name will be on their foreheads, Matt. 5:8; 1 Cor. 15:28; 1 John 3:2; Rev. 21:3; 22:4.
