Faith in special revelation is therefore ultimately one with faith in another and better world than this one. If this world, with the powers and laws naturally indwelling it, is the only and the best, then we must naturally make do with it. Then the _leges naturae_ are equal to the _decreta Dei_; then this world is the Son, the Logos, the proper image of God; then in the _ordo naturae_ in which we live there is already the full, adequate revelation of God’s wisdom and power, his goodness and holiness.

What right is there then to expect that the _Dort_ will nevertheless one day become _Hier_, that the ideal will pass into reality, that the good will triumph over evil, and that the _Welt der Werthe_ will one day rule over the _Welt der Wirklichkeit_? Evolution does not bring us there. _Nihil fit ex nihilo._ No paradise comes from this world. What does not lie within it cannot proceed from it. If there is no _Jenseits_, no God who stands above nature, no _ordo supernaturalis_, then sin, darkness, and death have the final word.

The revelation of Scripture makes known to us another world of holiness and glory that descends into this fallen world—not only as doctrine but also as divine δυναμις, as history, as reality, as a harmonious system of words and deeds together—and that raises this world from its fall and leads it from the _status peccati_ through the _status gratiae_ into the _status gloriae_. Revelation is God’s coming to humanity in order to dwell with it eternally.
