### C. Inspiration According to Scripture

## 9.

The Old Testament supplies the following important moments for the doctrine of inspiration.

a) The prophets know themselves to have been called by the Lord at a definite moment of their lives, Ex. 3; 1 Sam. 3; Isa. 6; Jer. 1; Ezek. 1–3; Amos 3:7, 8; 7:15. The call often went against their own wish and desire, Ex. 3; Jer. 20:7; Amos 3:8, but YHWH proved too strong for them. The conviction was universal among Israel that the prophets were messengers of God, Jer. 26:5; 7:15, raised up and sent by him, Jer. 29:15; Deut. 18:15; Num. 11:29; 2 Chron. 36:15; his servants, 2 Kings 17:23; 21:10; 24:2; Ezra 9:11; Ps. 105:15, etc.; standing before his face, 1 Kings 17:1; 2 Kings 3:14; 5:16.

b) They are conscious that YHWH has spoken to them and that they have received revelation from him. He teaches them what they shall speak, Ex. 2:12; Deut. 18:18; puts the words in their mouth, Num. 22:38; 23:5; Deut. 18:18; speaks to them, Hos. 1:2; Hab. 2:1; Zech. 1:9, 13; 2:2, 7; 4:1, 4, 11; 5:5, 10; 6:4; Num. 12:2, 8; 2 Sam. 23:2; 1 Kings 22:28. The formula especially used is: “Thus says the Lord,” or “The word of the Lord came to me,” or “word, oracle,” נאם, passive participle, that which was spoken by YHWH. The whole Old Testament Scripture is full of this expression. Again and again the prophetic address is introduced by it. YHWH himself is even repeatedly introduced as speaking in the first person, Josh. 24:2; Isa. 1:1, 2; 8:1, 11; Jer. 1:2, 4, 11; 2:1; 7:1; Ezek. 1:3; 2:1; Hos. 1:1; Joel 1:1; Amos 2:1, etc. Properly speaking, it is YHWH who speaks through them, 2 Sam. 23:1, 2; who speaks through their mouth, Ex. 4:12, 15; Num. 23:5; through their ministry, Hag. 1:1; 2 Kings 17:13. Their whole word is covered by YHWH’s authority.

c) This consciousness is so clear and firm among the prophets that they even indicate the place and time where YHWH spoke to them and distinguish between times when he did and did not speak to them, Isa. 16:13, 14; Jer. 3:6; 13:3; 26:1; 27:1; 28:1; 33:1; 34:1; 35:1; 36:1; 49:34; Ezek. 3:16; 8:1; 12:8; Hag. 1:1; Zech. 1:1, etc. Moreover, that consciousness is so objective that they clearly distinguish themselves from YHWH. He speaks to them, Isa. 8:1; 51:16; 59:21; Jer. 1:9; 3:6; 5:14; Ezek. 3:26, etc.; and they listen with their ears and see with their eyes, Isa. 5:9; 6:8; 21:3, 10; 22:14; 28:22; Jer. 23:18; 49:14; Ezek. 2:8; 3:10, 17; 33:7; 40:4; 44:5; Hab. 3:2, 16; 2 Sam. 7:27; Job 33:16; 36:10, and receive the words of YHWH into themselves, Jer. 15:16; Ezek. 3:1–3.

d) Hence they draw a sharp opposition between what God has revealed to them and what arises from their own heart, Num. 16:28; 24:13; 1 Kings 12:33; Neh. 6:8; Ps. 41:6, 7. They charge the false prophets precisely with speaking from their own heart, Ezek. 13:2, 3, 17; Jer. 14:14; 23:16, 26; Isa. 59:13, without having been sent, Jer. 14:14; 29:9; Ezek. 13:6, so that they are prophets of lies, Jer. 23:32; Isa. 9:14; Jer. 14:14; 20:6; 23:21, 22, 26, 31, 36; 27:14; Ezek. 13:6 f.; Mic. 2:11; Zeph. 3:4; Zech. 10:2, and soothsayers, Isa. 3:2; Mic. 3:5 f.; Zech. 10:2; Jer. 27:9; 29:8; Ezek. 13:9, 12; 21:26, 28, 34; Isa. 44:25. König II 163–167; Herzog² 16, 726.

e) Finally, the prophets are conscious that in speaking or writing they proclaim not their own word but the Word of the Lord. Indeed, the word was revealed to them not for themselves but for others. They had no freedom to conceal it. They _must_ speak, Jer. 20:7, 9; Ex. 3, 4; Ezek. 3; Amos 3:8; Jonah, and therefore do not speak according to human favor or calculation, Isa. 56:10; Mic. 3:5, 11. This is precisely why they are prophets, speakers in YHWH’s name and of his Word. They then know that they must give everything they have received, Deut. 4:2; 12:32; Jer. 1:7, 17; 26:2; 42:4; Ezek. 3:10.

The writing of the prophets may and must also be derived from the same impulse. The literal texts in which a command to write is given are few—Ex. 17:14; 24:3, 4; 34:27; Num. 33:2; Deut. 4:2; 12:32; 31:19; Isa. 8:1; 30:8; Jer. 25:13; 30:1; 36:2, 24, 27–32; Ezek. 24:1; Dan. 12:4; Hab. 2:2—and concern only a very small portion of the Old Testament Scripture. But written recording, though a later stage, is nevertheless a necessary stage in the history of prophetism. Many prophecies were certainly never spoken but were indeed intended to be read and pondered. Most are composed with care, even with artistry, and show by their very form that they were intended for writing. The recording of the divine oracles was guided by the thought that Israel could no longer be saved by deeds, that now and in distant generations the service of YHWH had to gain acceptance through word and rational conviction, Kuenen, _Prof._ I 74, II 345 f. They began to write because they wished to address others besides only those who could hear them.

f) There is a difference between the received word and the spoken or written word. There would be nothing degrading to the prophets if they had recorded the received word as literally as possible. But revelation continued also in the moment of theopneusty and modified and completed the earlier revelation, which was therefore freely reproduced. Precisely for this reason the prophets claim the same authority for their written word as for the spoken word. Even the parenthetical remarks of the prophets inserted among the proper words of YHWH—for example, Isa. 6; 10:24–12:6; 31:1–3; 32—or the prophet’s elaboration of a word of YHWH, 52:7–12; 63:15–64:12, form no exception to this. The transition from the Word of YHWH into the word of the prophet and conversely is often so sudden, and the two are so interwoven—for example, Jer. 13:18 f.—that separation is impossible. They possess the same authority, Jer. 36:10, 11; 25:3. Isaiah at 34:16 calls his own recorded prophecies the Book of YHWH.
