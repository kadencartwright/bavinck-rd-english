## 5.

Although sin is always one in principle and essence and consistently consists in ἀνομια, there are very different degrees in its manifestations and operations.
First of all, there is a great distinction between diabolical and human sin.
In the Old Testament we do not yet find a developed demonology.
That an evil spiritual power appears in Gen. 3 we know only from the New Testament; the ‎‏שְׂעִירִם‏‎, Lev. 17:7; 2 Chron. 11:15; Isa. 13:21; 34:14, ‎‏שֵׁדִים‏‎, Deut. 32:17; Ps. 106:27, ‎‏לִילִית‏‎, Isa. 34:14, and ‎‏עֲלוּקָה‏‎, Prov. 30:15, certainly are not to be regarded as elements of revelation; and that ‎‏עֲזָאזֵל‏‎, Lev. 16, must be understood as an evil spirit is incapable of proof.
Evil spirits are mentioned only in 1 Sam. 16:14–23 and 1 Kings 22:19 f., and Satan in Job 1; 1 Chron. 21:1; Zech. 3.
The separation between good and evil angels has not yet been completed; the evil spirit still proceeds from God, and Satan is still found among the sons of God; only gradually does the opposition become sharper.
The word _satan_ means adversary and can in itself have a good sense; it occurs of human opponents, 1 Sam. 29:4; 1 Kings 5:4; 11:14; 23:25; of hindrances on the way, 2 Sam. 19:23; of a human accuser, Ps. 109:6, 20, 29; and even of the Malak YHWH who confronts Balaam on the way, Num. 22:22, 32.
Nevertheless, Satan is already conceived in the Old Testament as a being who stands hostile over against God and his people.
And when revelation is completed and Christ comes to destroy the works of the devil, the βαθη του σατανα are also made manifest.
The New Testament introduces us to a βασιλεια, Matt. 12:26; Mark 3:24; Luke 11:17, 18, of evil spirits that forms the antithesis of Christ and his kingdom.
At its head stands Satan, called by various names: διαβολος, σατανας, ἐχθρος, Matt. 13:39; Luke 10:19; κατηγωρ, Rev. 12:10; βελιαρ (Syr. for βελιαλ, worthlessness); πονηρος, Matt. 13:19; Eph. 6:16; 2 Thess. 3:3; 1 John 2:13, 14; 3:12; 5:18; βεελζεβουλ (lit. lord of the dwelling, but probably derived from βεελζεβουβ, god of flies; art. Herzog³ 2, 514), Matt. 10:25; ἀρχων των δαιμονιων, Matt. 9:34; ἀρχων της ἐζουσιας του ἀερος, Eph. 2:2; ἀρχων του κοσμου, John 12:31; ὁ θεος του αἰωνος τουτου, 2 Cor. 4:4; ὁ δρακων ὁ μεγας, ὁ οφις ὁ ἀρχαιος, Rev. 12:9; 20:2, etc.

Although there is thus still some difference among them in strength and wickedness, they are nevertheless all represented as corrupt through and through.
Always and everywhere they are the adversaries of God, the disturbers of his kingdom, the opponents of Christ, the tempters of human beings, the accusers of God’s children; they live in sin as in their element.
They never appear as objects of God’s love, although they are his creatures; Christ did not assume their nature; they may not be the object of our love or of our intercession; for them there is no hope of restoration and salvation.

There is something entirely incomprehensible in the essence and concept of devils.
_Wir können des absolute böse Wesen immer nur unter der Bedingung denken, dass wir entweder an der absoluten Bosheit oder an der wahren Existenz etwas fehlen lassen_; C. J. Nitzsch, _Syst. d. chr. Lehr_ § 116; F. A. B. Nitzsch, _Ev. Dogm._, 337.
They cannot be absolutely evil, for they are creatures of God and thus as such are good; and yet they are solely objects of God’s hatred and his eternal wrath.
Because of this incomprehensibility of the nature of devils, many have denied their existence and regarded them as souls of deceased human beings, as personifications of our evil sins, or as impersonal _principia_ of evil; Bekker, _Betov. Werelt_ II c. 20 f.; Semler, _de daemoniacis_, 1760; Schleiermacher, _Chr. Gl._ § 44, 45; Schelling, _Werke_ II 4, S. 241 f.; Rothe, _Ethik_ § 503; Martensen, _Dogm._ § 99 f.; Mallet, art. “Teufel” in Herzog; Strauss, _Chr. Gl._ II 1 f.; Biedermann, _Dogm._, 614 f.; Lipsius, _Dogm._ § 521–524, etc.
But the reality of Satan and his angels is placed beyond doubt by Scripture; an appeal to accommodation is wholly out of the question; either Jesus was completely mistaken on a very weighty point of religion, or it is as he said.
And the doctrine of Satan is also far from indifferent to the whole of Christian doctrine.
It is of value over against Manichaeism, for Satan is not an original being but a fallen angel; over against Pelagianism, for Satan was entirely corrupted by a single decision of the will; over against the conception of sin as weakness and sensuousness, for Satan is a high, glorious, richly endowed spirit; over against the opinion that sin is a passing moment in development, for Satan remains Satan and is never restored; over against the degradation of the human being into a devil, for Satan fell of his own accord while the human being was seduced by him and, though not innocent (_un_schuldig), is not originally guilty (_ur_schuldig) either; over against the conception of atonement as an ethical process, for Christ came to destroy the works of the devil.
Belief in Satan’s existence is not an element of saving faith in Christ, but it is nevertheless connected with it.
There is truth in the saying: _nullus diabolus, nullus redemptor!_
If there were no sin, there would be no redeemer, and the seriousness of sin comes out most clearly precisely in the doctrine of Satan.
From everything Scripture testifies concerning the angels, it is evident that moral life among them bears a different character from that among human beings.
Human beings are subject to development in every sphere and thus also in the moral sphere; they are born small in knowledge, power, virtue, or vice, and gradually grow in all these.
But it is not so with the angels.
They were all created together and mature; those who remained standing were confirmed in the good at once, and those who fell were immediately hardened and consummated in evil.
Satan was not tempted, but he brought forth the lie, sin, from himself, John 8:44, and in it became obdurate at once.
The nature of his sin is such that he is no longer capable of repentance; there is no question in him of moral consciousness, of a conscience; he lives by hatred.
_Der eigentlich satanische Charakter besteht in einem Hass alles dessen, was über ihm und bloss weil es über ihm ist_ (Baader).
Sin even in the devils is not _materia_ but _forma_; there is no _summum malum_ as there is a _summum bonum_; but the _forma_ of sin has become so united with the angelic nature that separation is no longer possible.
It is admittedly far too bold to assert that the fallen angels are unredeemable even by God’s omnipotence; and it is better here to rest in God’s good pleasure; Voetius, _Disp._ I 920; Turretinus, _Th. El._ IX 5, 8; Heidegger, _Corp. Th._ VIII 49; Moor II 414.
Yet it is sufficiently evident that this good pleasure is no arbitrariness.
Here on earth there is already among human beings a sin that is unforgivable, namely, blasphemy against the Holy Spirit; with death, that is, with the end of that distinctive dispensation in which we live here on earth, the forgiveness of all sins ceases; the nature of sin cuts off the way of redemption for the fallen angels.
To this is added that the angels are not one race.
Human beings could fall and did fall in one; and they can be saved and are saved in one.
But the devils did not fall in one, nor in another, but each for himself and one by one; there was no _foedus operum_ among them, and therefore there is also no place for a _foedus gratiae_.
Satanic sin is therefore, despite every similarity, entirely different from human sin in origin, nature, and consequences.
It bears an absolute character; Satan is the highest manifestation of evil.
Therefore he is called in Scripture by such mighty, exalted names as ruler of the world, god of this age, and so forth.
But for this reason the victory over Satan is also the complete triumph over sin.
In Satan God gave sin every opportunity to show what it is and what it can do.
It made the highest and best, the noblest and greatest in God’s creation, serviceable to itself.
And yet in the end, in the struggle of might against right, it proves to be powerless.
_Es ist der Charakter des Bösen, dass es immer mit Energie anfängt und mit Schwäche aufhört_ (Baader).
Sin is not; it wills to be; it has no true reality and never attains it; it is a lie in its origin and a lie in its end.
And therefore Satan, with all his power, ultimately serves the glorification of God.
_Luzifer ist, kann man sagen, durch die Probe inne geworden, dass nichts wahrhaft ist als Gott. Darum ist Luzifer so gut ein Beweis Gottes als ein Engel. Wenn der Gute beweist, dass Gott ist, so beweist der Böse, dass nur Gott ist_ (Baader).
