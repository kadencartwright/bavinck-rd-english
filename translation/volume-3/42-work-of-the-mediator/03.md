## 3.

According to the New Testament, all these various testimonies of law and prophecy culminate in Christ. The entire Old Testament is fulfilled in principle in him. In him all God’s promises are yes and amen, Rom. 15:8; 2 Cor. 1:20. He is the true Messiah, the king from David’s house, Matt. 2:2; 21:5; 27:11, 37; Luke 1:32, and so forth; the prophet who proclaims the gospel to the poor, Luke 4:17 f.; the priest who in his person, office, appointment, sacrifice, and sanctuary far surpasses the Old Testament priesthood, Hebrews; the servant of the Lord who as a δουλος, Phil. 2:7, 8, came to serve, Mark 10:45, submitted himself to the law, Gal. 4:4, fulfilled all righteousness, Matt. 3:15, and was obedient to the death of the cross, Rom. 5:19; Phil. 2:8; Heb. 5:8. As such, Jesus now distinguishes between the kingdom of God as it is presently established by him in a spiritual sense and as it will one day be revealed in glory; between his first and second coming, which still coincided for Old Testament prophecy; between his work in the state of humiliation and that in the state of exaltation. The Christ must enter into his glory through suffering, Luke 24:26. The work he now accomplishes in the state of humiliation is described in many ways in the New Testament. It is an ἐργον assigned to him by the Father, John 4:34; 5:36; 17:4. In general it consisted in doing God’s will, Matt. 26:42; John 4:34; 5:30; 6:38, and more specifically encompassed the explanation of God, John 1:18; the revelation and glorification of his name, 17:4, 6, 26; the communication of God’s words, 17:8, 14, and so forth. Christ is a prophet mighty in words and works, Luke 24:19. He is no _novus legislator_, but he explains the law, Matt. 5–7; 22:40; Luke 9:23; 10:28; John 13:34; 1 John 2:7, 8; proclaims the gospel, Matt. 12:16–21; Luke 4:17–21; and in both preaches himself as the fulfiller of the law and content of the gospel. He is the law and the gospel in his own person. He is a prophet not merely through what he says but first of all through what he is. He is the Logos, John 1:1, full of grace and truth, John 1:18, anointed with the Spirit without measure, 3:34, the revelation of the Father, John 14:9; Col. 2:9. He himself is the source of his preaching; its source is not inspiration but incarnation. God’s Spirit did not merely come upon him. God did not merely speak with him face to face as with Moses, but God was in him and spoke through him, Heb. 1:3. He is not a prophet alongside others but the highest, the only prophet, source and center of all prophecy. All knowledge of God, in the Old Testament before his incarnation and also now in the New Testament after his resurrection and ascension, is from him, 1 Pet. 1:11; 3:19; Matt. 11:27. Next, God’s will that Jesus came to accomplish also encompassed the miracles he performed. The one work divides into many ἐργα, John 5:36. Those works are his Father’s, 5:20; 9:3; 10:32, 37; 14:10; prove that the Father loves him and is in him, 5:20; 10:38; 14:10; testify that the Father sent him, 5:36; 10:25; and reveal his divine glory, 2:11; 11:4, 40. Indeed, he not only performs miracles, but in his person is himself the absolute miracle. As the incarnate, conceived by the Holy Spirit, risen, and glorified Son of God, he is himself the greatest miracle, the center of all miracles, author of the re-creation of all things, firstborn from the dead, the First in all things, Col. 1:18. Further, God’s will especially encompasses that the Father’s only-begotten Son lay down his life for his own, John 10:18. The New Testament sees in Christ a sacrifice and the fulfillment of Old Testament sacrificial worship. He is the true covenant sacrifice. Just as the old covenant was confirmed by the covenant sacrifice, Ex. 24:3–11, so Christ’s blood is the blood of the new testament, Matt. 26:28; Mark 14:24; Heb. 9:13 f. Christ is a θυσια, זֶבַח, the sacrificial victim for our sins, Eph. 5:2; Heb. 9:26; 10:12; a προσφορα, δωρον, קָרְבָּן, מִנְתָה, Eph. 5:2; Heb. 10:10, 14, 18; a λυτρον, ἀντιλυτρον, Matt. 20:28; Mark 10:45; 1 Tim. 2:6; גְּאֻלָּה, פָדוּי, כֹפֶר, ransom, redemption price, to buy someone free from captivity, and hence an atoning means, to cover another’s sin through a sacrifice and so rescue that person from death; a τιμη, 1 Cor. 6:20; 7:23; 1 Pet. 1:18, 19, a price paid for redemption; a sin offering made sin for us, 2 Cor. 5:21; 1 John 2:2; 4:10; the Passover sacrifice slaughtered for us, John 19:36; 1 Cor. 5:7; the Lamb of God who bears the sin of the world and was slaughtered for it, John 1:29, 36; Acts 8:32; 1 Pet. 1:19; Rev. 5:6, and so forth; the ἱλαστηριον, Rom. 3:25—that is, not the mercy seat, כַפֹּרֶת, although this word was so translated in the Septuagint because no better equivalent could be found in Greek, but that which serves for atonement, an atoning means, or with θυμα understood, atoning sacrifice; cf. Deissmann, _Die sprachl. Forschung der griech. Bibel_, Giessen, 1898, pp. 16, 17; a καταρα, Gal. 3:13, who took over from us the curse of the law; like the serpent in the wilderness, lifted up on the cross, John 3:14; 8:28; 12:33; and like a grain of wheat dying in the earth in order thus to bear much fruit, John 12:24. The relation in which this sacrifice of Christ stands to us and our sin is likewise expressed in very diverse ways. He gives or lays down his life, Mark 10:45; John 10:15; sanctifies himself, John 17:19; is made sin and curse, 2 Cor. 5:21; Gal. 3:13; is delivered up, Rom. 4:25; gave himself, Gal. 2:20; suffered, 1 Pet. 3:18; was crucified and died, John 11:50, 51; Rom. 5:6; 1 Cor. 15:3; 2 Cor. 5:15; 1 Thess. 5:10. And this indeed as a λυτρον ἀντι πολλων, Matt. 20:28; Mark 10:45, in the place of many; or ὑπερ with the genitive of the person, John 10:15; 11:50, 51; Rom. 5:6, 8; 2 Cor. 5:15, 21; Gal. 3:13; Eph. 5:2; Heb. 2:9, for our benefit, for the benefit of his people; cf. Philem. 13, ὑπερ σου, _in gratiam tuam_, so that you do not need to do it; or ὑπερ with the genitive of the thing, John 6:51; 1 Cor. 15:3; Heb. 10:12, for the benefit of sins, to put them away, or for the benefit of the life of the world, so that through Christ’s death it might obtain life; or περι with the genitive of the person, Matt. 26:28; 1 John 2:2, for, for the sake of many or of the whole world; or περι with the genitive of the thing, Rom. 8:3; Heb. 10:6, 18; 1 Pet. 3:18; 1 John 2:2; 4:10, for, for the sake of sin; or δια with the accusative of the thing, Rom. 4:25, on account of, because of sin.

What Christ acquired through this sacrifice of his is almost too much to enumerate. For himself he thereby acquired his whole exaltation: the resurrection, Eph. 1:20; the ascension, 1 Pet. 3:22; the session at God’s right hand, Eph. 1:20; Heb. 12:2; elevation to Head of the church, Eph. 1:22; the name above every name, Phil. 2:9–11; the glory of the Mediator, Heb. 2:9; dominion over all things in heaven and on earth, Matt. 28:18; Eph. 1:22; 1 Cor. 15:24 f.; the last judgment, John 5:22, 27. And further, for his own, for humanity, for the world, he acquired an endless series of blessings. In his person he is himself the sum of all these blessings: the light of the world, John 8:12; the true bread, 6:35; the true vine, 15:1; the way, the truth, the resurrection, and the life, 11:25; 14:6; our σοφια, δικαιοσυνη, ἁγιασμος, ἀπολυτρωσις, 1 Cor. 1:30; our ειρηνη, Eph. 2:14; the firstborn and firstfruits who is followed by many, Rom. 8:29; 1 Cor. 15:23; the second and last Adam, 1 Cor. 15:45; the Head of the church, Eph. 1:22; the cornerstone of God’s building, Eph. 2:20. There is therefore no participation in his benefits except through fellowship with his person. Yet from him all benefits flow: the whole σωτηρια, Matt. 1:21; Luke 2:11; John 3:17; 12:47; and then more specifically the forgiveness of sins, Matt. 26:28; Eph. 1:7; the taking away, αἰρειν, of our sins, John 1:29; 1 John 3:5; the cleansing or liberation from an evil conscience, Heb. 10:22; justification, Rom. 4:25; righteousness, 1 Cor. 1:30; υἱοθεσια, Gal. 3:26; 4:5, 6; Eph. 1:5; confident access to God, Eph. 2:18; 3:12; God’s laying aside his wrath on the ground of Christ’s sacrifice, that is, ἱλασμος, Rom. 3:25; 1 John 2:2; 4:10; Heb. 2:17; the new, reconciled relation of peace toward the world that with God took the place of that wrath, a relation no longer hostile but favorable, καταλλαγη, Rom. 5:10 f.; 2 Cor. 5:18–20; and the human being’s relation of peace toward God, Rom. 5:1. Further, the gift of the Holy Spirit, John 15:26; Acts 2; Gal. 4:6; regeneration and childship from God, John 1:12, 13; sanctification, 1 Cor. 1:30; fellowship with Christ’s death, Rom. 6:3 f.; dying to sin, Rom. 6:6 f.; Gal. 2:20; crucifixion to the world, Gal. 6:14; cleansing, Eph. 5:26; 1 John 1:7, 9; and washing away, 1 Cor. 6:11; Rev. 1:5; 7:14, of sins through sprinkling with the blood of Christ, Heb. 9:22; 12:24; 1 Pet. 1:2; walking in the Spirit and in newness of life, Rom. 6:4; participation in Christ’s resurrection and ascension, Rom. 6:5; Eph. 2:6; Phil. 3:20; following Christ, Matt. 10:38; 1 Pet. 2:21 f. Further still, liberation from the curse of the law, Rom. 6:14; 7:1–6; Gal. 3:13; Col. 2:14; fulfillment of the old covenant and inauguration of a new covenant, Mark 14:24; Heb. 7:22; 9:15; 12:24; deliverance from the power of Satan, Luke 11:22; John 14:30; Col. 2:15; 1 John 3:8; Col. 1:13; victory over the world, John 16:33; 1 John 4:4; 5:4; liberation from death and the fear of death, Rom. 5:12 f.; 1 Cor. 15:55 f.; Heb. 2:15; escape from judgment, Heb. 10:27, 28. And finally, the resurrection on the last day, John 11:25; 1 Cor. 15:21; ascension, Eph. 2:6; glorification, John 17:24; the heavenly inheritance, John 14:1; 1 Pet. 1:4; eternal life, already beginning here with faith, John 3:15, 36, and one day manifesting itself fully in glory, Mark 10:30; Rom. 6:22; the new heaven and earth, 2 Pet. 3:13; Rev. 21:1, 5; the restoration of all things, Acts 3:21; 1 Cor. 15:24–28.
