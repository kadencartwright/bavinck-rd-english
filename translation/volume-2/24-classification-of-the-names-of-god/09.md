## 9.

All the classifications mentioned above appear to diverge widely from one another and employ entirely different names. But materially they agree very closely. Whether one speaks of negative and positive, incommunicable and communicable, quiescent and operative, absolute and relative attributes, of attributes of the substance and of the subject, in relation to the world and to the human being, one in fact always indicates the same order in which the attributes are treated. In the main, therefore, the same objections apply to all the classifications. They all give the appearance that God’s essence is divided into two halves; that absoluteness is treated first and personality afterward; that the essence _an sich_ is discussed first and then in its relation to creatures; that the first group of names is obtained apart from creation and the second group from creatures; and that in this way no unity and harmony of all God’s excellencies is achieved, Frank, _Syst. d. chr. Wahrheit_ I 228 f. It is, however, the incontrovertible teaching of Scripture that God is unknowable and unnameable in his hidden essence and that all God’s names presuppose revelation—that is, creation. We know nothing of God’s being and life outside and apart from creation, for the simple reason that we ourselves are creatures and therefore always bound to creation. Earlier this was clearly recognized and expressed with respect to the relative, metaphorical, and positive names of God. The relative names such as Lord, Creator, Preserver, Savior, and so forth, belong to God only after and through creation; no one can be called lord unless he has servants. Human beings, God’s servants, came to be in time, and thus God became our Lord in time, Augustine, _De trin._ V 16; cf. _De ord._ II 7; Thomas, _S. Theol._ I, qu. 13, art. 7; Anselm, _Monol._ 15; Lombard, _Sent._ I, dist. 30; Bonaventure, _Sent._ I, dist. 30, art. 1; Zanchi, _Op._ II 24–26; Polanus, _Synt. Theol._, p. 192. It is clear of the metaphorical names, to which the anthropomorphisms also belong, that they presuppose creation. And the positive names good, holy, wise, and so forth, have some content for our consciousness because we perceive ectypes of them in creatures, Thomas, _S. Theol._ I, qu. 12, art. 12. Yet, on the other hand, all these names, though relative, metaphorical, and positive, do indeed make known something in God that exists in him absolutely, _proprie_, and thus also negatively—in a different sense than in creatures. Augustine attempted to demonstrate that although God becomes Lord in time, his essence is nevertheless immutable and all change occurs only in the creature. _Quod temporaliter dici incipit Deus, quod antea non dicebatur, manifestum est relative dici; non tamen secundum accidens Dei quod ei aliquid acciderit, sed plane secundum accidens ejus, ad quod dici aliquid Deus incipit relative_, _De trin._ V 16. The converse applies to the second group of names, whether these are called negative, incommunicable, quiescent, absolute, or something else. For although they deny of God something that is in creatures, they are nevertheless all in a certain sense also positive, communicable, transeunt, relative. If they were not, if they were completely incommunicable, they would also be absolutely unknowable and unnameable. The fact that we can think and name them proves that in one way or another they have been revealed by God in his works. The negative names therefore also have a positive content. Although we learn to know God’s eternity only by and in time, his omnipresence by and in space, his infinity and immutability only by and amid all finite and mutable creatures, they nevertheless give us some—and even important—knowledge of God. Although we cannot comprehend eternity positively, it nevertheless says so much to know that God is exalted above all time. It is therefore incorrect to classify God’s attributes in such a way that on one side there stands a group that is negative, incommunicable, quiescent, absolute, and so forth, and on the other side one that is positive, communicable, operative, relative, and so forth. For _all_ attributes are both at once. All attributes are absolute, and yet all are known only from God’s relation to his creatures. And conversely, we can name God only according to what of him is revealed in his creatures, yet we name him who is the source of every good and the cause of all creatures. More than that, precisely because the attributes are identical with God’s essence and with one another in God, no classification can be found that would be grounded objectively in the attributes themselves, Doedes, _Leer v. God_, p. 217. All that can be obtained is a certain order in treating the many names of God. Among these, those names immediately distinguish themselves which later were designated more specifically as names and which, as names of address and designation, may in a broader sense be called _nomina propria_. Next there are names that make known the divine essence in all its relations to creatures and that formerly were usually called attributes, excellencies, perfections, and so forth. The word attributes is least preferable for these because it repeatedly suggests an essence to which the attributes are added; the name excellencies, by contrast, has support in Scripture itself, 1 Pet. 2:9. This second group can bear the name _nomina essentialia_ because they all make known to us God’s essence, not _an sich_, but as it descends to us in revelation and communicates itself to us. Finally, there is a third group of names, all of which concern the self-distinctions in God’s essence and can therefore be called _nomina personalia_. The most difficult matter now is to bring order into the treatment of the names belonging to the second group. Yet that order is indicated precisely in the relation in which God stands to his creatures. The whole world is a revelation of God. There is no part of the universe in which something of his excellencies is not revealed. But there is distinction among creatures. Not all proclaim all his excellencies, nor do all proclaim them with the same clarity. There is rank and ascent: all creatures display _vestigia Dei_, but only the human being is the image and likeness of God. He shares not only being with the lower creatures and life and spirit with the higher, but in this fellowship with the material and spiritual world he is also related in an entirely special way to God himself, created as prophet, priest, and king in knowledge, holiness, and righteousness. Thus God, the source of all being and the archetype of the human being, is also himself all that in which creatures participate. In him all this indeed exists in an original, divine manner; in every excellence of God both his absolute exaltation above and his affinity with the creature may be observed. Every attribute is incommunicable in one respect and communicable again in another. Yet there is an analogy from the creature, especially from the human being, in the divine essence. There are therefore, first, names by which God reveals himself to us as the Being in the absolute sense: _aseitas, immutabilitas, infinitas_ (_aeternitas_ and _immensitas_). Second, there are names that make God known to us as the Living One, as Spirit: _spiritualitas, invisibilitas, simplicitas_. Third, there are names that describe the divine essence as perfectly self-conscious, as Light: _scientia, sapientia, veracitas_. Fourth, there are names that point us to the ethical nature of that essence and designate God as the Holy One: _bonitas, justitia, sanctitas_. And finally, fifth, there are names in which God appears to us as absolute power, as Lord, King, Sovereign: _voluntas, libertas, omnipotentia_. This classification is connected, on the one hand, with the knowledge of God summarized in the so-called proofs for his existence and, on the other hand, points forward to the _vestigia_ and the _imago Dei_ impressed in creatures. No knowledge of God except through revelation, and therefore always analogical and ectypal; but through that revelation nevertheless true and pure knowledge of God’s incomprehensible and adorable essence!
