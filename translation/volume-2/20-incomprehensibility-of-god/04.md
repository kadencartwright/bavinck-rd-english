## 4.

This unknowability of God’s essence was taught still more strongly by Pseudo-Dionysius, upon whom Damascene already appeals, and by Scotus Erigena. According to the Areopagite there is no concept, expression, or word that expresses God’s essence, _De div. nom._, c. 1, §1. God is therefore designated with unusual, figurative names. He is ὑπερουσιος ἀοριστια, _suprasubstantialis infinitas_, ἡ ὑπερ νῶν ἑνοτης, _supra mentem unitas_, το ὑπερ διανοιαν ἑν, _illud supra ratiocinationem unum_, ὑπερουσιος οὐσια και νους ἀνοητος και λογος ἀρρητος, ἀλογια και ἀνοησια και ἀνωνυμια, κατα μηδεν των ὀντων οὐσα, ibid. We can neither express nor think that one, unknown being, exalted above being, above the good, above every name and word and understanding, above everything finite, ibid., c. 1, §5. Only because he is the cause and principle of everything can we, like Scripture, name him according to his operations, ibid., c. 1, §4. He is therefore on the one hand ἀνωνυμος and on the other πολυωνυμος, ibid., 1, §6. But even the positive names we attribute to God on the ground of his works do not make him known to us in his essence, for they belong to him in an entirely different and infinitely more perfect manner than to creatures, _De myst. theol._, c. 1, §2. Negative theology is therefore more excellent than positive theology, for it makes God known to us as exalted above every creature. Yet it too furnishes us no knowledge of God’s essence. For ultimately God surpasses both every negation and every affirmation, every θεσις and every ἀφαιρεσις, _De myst. theol._, c. 5. Erigena reasons entirely in the same spirit. God is exalted above everything creaturely, including being and knowing, _De div. nat._ I 7 sq.; II 23. We know only that he is, not what he is, ibid., I 13; II 28; V 26. What we predicate of him, he is only in an improper manner, and so in turn he is also not that, I 36. Affirmative theology is improper, metaphorical, I 14 sq., 37. It is surpassed by negative theology. _Verius enim negatur Deus quid eorum, quod de eo praedicatur, quam affirmatur esse_, I 76; II 30; III 20. _Melius nesciendo scitur, cujus ignorantia vera est sapientia_, I 66. His predicates are therefore best supplemented by _super_ or _plusquam_. He is _superessentialis, plusquam veritas, plusquam sapientia_, and so forth, I 14. Indeed, he so far surpasses everything creaturely that he can not improperly be designated by the name nothing, _nihilum_, III 19. Cf. Stöckl, _Philos. des M. A._ I 45 f.; Baur, _Die christl. Lehre von der Dreiein. und Menschw. Gottes_ II 274 f.; Doedes, _Inl. tot de leer van God_, 133 f.
