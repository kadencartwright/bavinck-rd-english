## 2.

Even outside the sphere of special revelation this majesty of God has been acknowledged.
Among the Brahmins God is the Unknowable, without names and attributes, known only by the one who does not know, Hoekstra, _Wĳsg. Godsd._ II 2; Hartmann, _Religionsphilos._ I 278 f.
The Quran frequently describes Allah in a very anthropomorphic manner, but among the followers of Muhammad many arose who interpreted this description spiritually and even denied all attributes to God, Dozy, _Het Islamisme_, p. 131 f.; Houtsma, _De strĳd over het dogma in den Islam tot op El-Ash’ari_, 1875, p. 120 f.; Saussaye, _Lehrb. der Rel._ II 385.
Greek philosophy too repeatedly acknowledged and expressed clearly this unknowability of God.
According to the well-known story, the philosopher Simonides repeatedly requested a longer delay before answering the question of the tyrant Hiero as to who God was, Cicero, _De nat. deor._ I 22.
According to Diogenes, Protagoras began his book on the gods as follows: περι μεν θεων οὐκ ἐχω εἰδεναι οὐδ’ ὡς εἰσιν οὐδ’ ὡς οὐκ εἰσιν πολλα γαρ τα κωλυοντα εἰδεναι, ἡ τ’ ἀδηλοτης και βραχυς ὠν ὁ βιος των ἀνθρωπων, Ritter et Preller, _Hist. phil. gr._ 183; Cicero, _De nat. deor._ I 63.
Carneades of Cyrene not only subjected belief in the gods to sharp criticism but also contested the possibility of forming a conception of God.
Plato rejected every anthropomorphic and anthropopathic representation of the deity and declared in the _Timaeus_ § 34: τον μεν οὐν ποιητην και πατερα τονδε του παντος εὑρειν τε ἐργον και εὑροντα εἰς παντας ἀδυνατον λεγειν, Zeller, _Philos. der Gr._ II 4, 928 f.
And in the same sense he says in the _Republic_ VI 19 that the deity, or the Idea of the Good, is exalted not only above all that exists but also above being itself, ἐπεκεινα πασες οὐσιας.
Philo joined this Platonic philosophy with the doctrine of the Old Testament and found the same thought expressed in the name YHWH.
God is not only free from every imperfection present in finite, mutable, dependent creatures; no, God also far surpasses every perfection of creatures.
He is better than virtue, knowledge, and beauty, purer than unity, more blessed than blessedness.
Properly speaking, he is without qualities, ἀποιος, and without names, and therefore can neither be comprehended nor described.
In his essence God is unknowable.
We can know that he is, but not what he is.
Being alone can in the proper sense be ascribed to him; the name YHWH is the only one that indicates his essence, Zeller, _Philos. d. Gr._ V³ 353 f.; Dähne, _Gesch. Darstellung der jüdisch-alex. Religionsphilos._, 1834, I 114 f.; Schürer, _Neutest. Zeitgesch._, 1874, S. 655 f.
Plotinus went the furthest.
Plato still ascribed many attributes to God.
Philo supplemented his negative theology with a positive theology in which he describes God as a personal, almighty, perfect being.
But in Plotinus nothing can be said of God except negatively.
God is absolutely one, exalted above all plurality, and therefore cannot be described by thought, the Good, or even being, for all these determinations still include plurality.
God as pure unity is indeed the cause of thought, being, and the Good, but he is himself distinct from them and stands transcendent above them.
He is unbounded, infinite, without form, and so distinct from every creature that even activity, life, thought, consciousness, and being cannot be ascribed to him.
He is inaccessible to our thought and our language.
We cannot say what he is, but only what he is not.
Even the designations το ἑν and το ἀγαθον, which Plotinus generally employs, are no description of God’s essence but only of his relation to creatures, and indicate nothing other than his absolute causality, Zeller, ibid., 476–496.
The distance between God and his creature became still greater in Gnosticism.
It made an absolute separation between the highest God and the world.
In nature, in Israel, and in Christianity there was no essential revelation of God but only of the aeons.
There was therefore no _theologia naturalis_, whether _insita_ or _acquisita_, and likewise no _theologia revelata_.
The highest God was absolutely unknowable and unapproachable for the creature; he was βυθος ἀγνωστος, ἀρρητος, eternal σιγη, Irenaeus, _Adv. haer._ I 11, 24.
