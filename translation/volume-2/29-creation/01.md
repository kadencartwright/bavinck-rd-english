# Chapter V. The World in Its Original State

# § 29. Creation

## 1.

The execution of God’s counsel begins with creation.
Creation is the beginning and foundation of all God’s revelation and therefore also the foundation of all religious and ethical life.
The Old Testament creation narrative is of such sublime beauty that it is nowhere equaled and has been praised with one accord by everyone, including natural scientists such as Cuvier and von Humboldt.
_Das erste Blatt der mosaischen Urkunde hat mehr Gewicht als alle Folianten der Naturforscher und Philosophen_ (Jean Paul).
And thereafter this creation repeatedly comes to the fore in the history of revelation.
From the very first moment, true religion distinguishes itself from all other religions by conceiving the relation of God to the world and humanity as that of the Creator to his creature.
The thought of a being outside of and independent from God occurs nowhere in Scripture.
God is the sole and absolute cause of all that exists.
He created everything through his Word and Spirit, Gen. 1:2, 3; Ps. 33:6; 104:29; 148:5; Job 27:3; 33:4; Isa. 40:13; 48:13; Zech. 12:1; John 1:3; Col. 1:16; Heb. 1:2, and so forth.
Nothing stood over against him: no matter that binds him, no force that limits him.
He speaks and it is, Gen. 1:3; Ps. 33:9; Rom. 4:17.
He is the unlimited possessor of heaven and earth, Gen. 14:19, 22; Ps. 24:2; 89:12; 95:4, 5.
There is no limit to his power; he does whatever pleases him, Isa. 14:24, 27; 46:10; 55:10; Ps. 115:3; 135:6.
All things are from him and through him and to him, Rom. 11:36; 1 Cor. 8:6; Heb. 11:3.
The world is the product of his will, Ps. 33:6; Rev. 4:11; it is the revelation of his attributes, Prov. 8:22f.; Job 28:23f.; Ps. 104:1; 136:5f.; Jer. 16:12, and has its end in his honor, Isa. 43:17; Prov. 16:4; Rom. 11:36; 1 Cor. 8:6.
This doctrine of creation, which occupies such an all-dominating place in Scripture, is not presented as a philosophical explanation of the world problem.
It certainly also gives an answer to the question of the origin of all things; but above all it has a religious-ethical significance.
No right relation to God is conceivable except upon its foundation.
It places us in that relation to God in which we ought to stand.
And therefore it is of eminent practical value.
It serves to bring out the greatness, omnipotence, and majesty, and at the same time the goodness, wisdom, and love of God, Ps. 19; Job 37; Isa. 40; and it therefore strengthens faith, confirms trust in God, and comforts in suffering, Ps. 33:6 f.; 65:6f.; 89:12; 121:2; 134:5; Isa. 37:16; 40:28f.; 42:5, and so forth; it arouses praise and thanksgiving, Ps. 136:3f.; 148:5; Rev. 14:7; disposes us to humility and lowliness, and causes humanity to feel its smallness and insignificance before God, Job 38:4f.; Isa. 29:16; 45:9; Jer. 18:6; Rom. 9:20.
