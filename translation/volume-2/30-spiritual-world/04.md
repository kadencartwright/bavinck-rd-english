## 4.

Their unity appears first of all in the fact that they are all created beings.
Schelling indeed says that the good angels, as pure _Potenzen_, were not created, _Werke_ II 4, S. 284.
But the creation of the angels is clearly stated in Col. 1:16 and is included in the creation of all things, Gen. 1:1; Ps. 33:6; John 1:3; Rom. 11:36; Eph. 3:9; Heb. 1:2.
Little, however, can be said with certainty concerning the time of their creation.
With an appeal to Job 38:7, many church fathers thought that the angels were created before all things, Origen on Gen. 1; Basil, _Hexaem._, hom. 1; Gregory of Nazianzus, _Orat._ 38 and 42; John of Damascus, _De fide orthod._ II c. 3; Dionysius, _De div. nom._ c. 5, and so forth.
Later the Socinians judged in this way, Crell, _De Deo et attrib._ I c. 18, and the Remonstrants, Episcopius, _Inst. Theol._ IV 3, 1; Limborch II 20, 4, who in this way weakened the distinction between the Logos and the angels.
But this thought finds no support in Scripture.
Nothing precedes the creation of heaven and earth of which Gen. 1:1 speaks.
Job 38:7 indeed teaches that they were present at creation just as the stars were, but not that they already existed before the beginning of creation.
On the other hand, it is certain that the angels were created before the seventh day, when heaven and earth and all their host were completed and God rested from his labor, Gen. 1:31; 2:1, 2.
For the rest, however, we remain uncertain.
Only this may be regarded as probable: just as the earth in Gen. 1:1 is indeed already created as earth but must nevertheless still be prepared and adorned, so too heaven was not completed in one single moment.
The word _heaven_ in verse 1 is proleptic.
Only later in the history of revelation does it become evident what lies enclosed in it.
Scripture speaks at one time of heaven as the heaven of clouds, Gen. 1:8, 20; 7:11; Matt. 6:26; then as the starry heaven, Deut. 4:19; Ps. 8:4; Matt. 24:29; and finally as the dwelling of God and the angels, Pss. 115:16; 2:4; 1 Kings 8:27; 2 Chron. 6:18; Matt. 6:19-21; Heb. 4:14; 7:26; 8:1, 2; 9:2 f., and so forth.
Just as heaven as the heaven of clouds and stars was first prepared in the course of the six days, so it is possible and even probable that the third heaven with its inhabitants was also formed only gradually.
The richer and fuller that spiritual world is conceived, far surpassing the material world in diversity, the more plausible it is to posit a certain passage of time for this preparation of the heavens as well, even though the creation account is completely silent about it.
