## 2.

The names by which God is named and addressed by us are therefore not arbitrary; they were not invented by us at our pleasure. It is God himself who consciously and freely reveals himself in nature and grace, who grants us the right to name him on the ground of this revelation, indeed who in his Word has made his own names known to us on the ground of his revelation. All these names without distinction now bear this character, that they are derived from revelation. There is no single name that designates God’s essence _an sich_. The _nomen editum_ is the foundation of all _nomina indita_. And because God’s revelation in nature and Scripture is specifically directed to human beings, it is in human language that God speaks of himself to us; it is human words that he employs; it is human forms under which he appears. Thus anthropomorphisms do not occur here and there in Scripture; the whole of Scripture is anthropomorphic. From its first to its last page it testifies to God’s coming to and seeking of man. The whole revelation of God concentrates in the Logos who became σαρξ and is, as it were, one humanization, one incarnation of God. If God spoke to us in a divine manner, no creature would understand him; but this is his grace, which already began with creation, that he stoops down to his creatures and speaks to them and appears to them in a human manner. All the names by which God calls himself and permits us to call him are therefore derived from earthly and human relations. Thus in Scripture he is called El, the Strong One; El Shaddai, the Mighty One; YHWH, the One who Is; further, Father, Son, Spirit, good, merciful, gracious, righteous, holy, and so forth—all concepts that first apply to creatures and are then transferred to God in an eminent sense. Even the so-called incommunicable attributes, such as immutability, independence, simplicity, eternity, and omnipresence, are presented in Scripture in forms and expressions derived from the finite and therefore also in a negative sense; eternity cannot be represented except as a negation of time. Scripture makes not even a single attempt to define these perfections of God positively according to their own essence, abstracted from their relation to the finite. But anthropomorphism goes much further still in Scripture. Everything proper to human beings and even to creatures is also attributed to God, especially _partes hominis, membra corporis, sensus, affectus, actiones, subjecta_, and _adjuncta humana_. God has a soul, Lev. 26:11; Matt. 12:28, and a Spirit, Gen. 1:2, and so forth. A body of God is never spoken of, although in Christ God also assumed a true body, John 1:14; Col. 2:17, and the congregation is called Christ’s body, Eph. 1:22. Yet all bodily organs are attributed to God. His face is spoken of, Ex. 33:20, 23; Isa. 63:9; Ps. 16:11; Matt. 18:10; Rev. 22:4; his eyes, Ps. 11:4; Heb. 4:13; his eyelids, Ps. 11:4; the apple of his eye, Deut. 32:10; Ps. 17:8; Zech. 2:8; his ears, Ps. 55:2; his nose, Deut. 33:10; his mouth, Deut. 8:3; his lips, Job 11:5; his tongue, Isa. 30:27; his neck, Jer. 18:17; his arm, Ex. 15:16; his hand, Num. 11:23; his right hand, Ex. 15:12; his finger, Ex. 8:19; his heart, Gen. 6:6; his inward parts, Isa. 63:15; Jer. 31:20; Luke 1:78; his bosom, Ps. 74:4; his lap, John 1:28; his foot, Isa. 66:1. Further, there is no human emotion that is not also present in God, such as gladness, Isa. 62:5; rejoicing, Isa. 65:19; pain, Ps. 78:40; Isa. 63:10; grief, Ps. 95:10; Jer. 7:18, 19; fear, Deut. 32:27; love with all its modifications, such as mercy, compassion, grace, longsuffering, and so forth; further, zeal and jealousy, Deut. 32:21; repentance, Gen. 6:6; hatred, Deut. 16:22; wrath, Ps. 2:5; vengeance, Deut. 32:35. Further, all human actions too are transferred to God, such as taking cognizance, Gen. 18:21; searching, Ps. 7:10; knowing, Gen. 3:5; thinking, Gen. 50:20; forgetting, 1 Sam. 1:11; remembering, Gen. 8:1; Ex. 2:24; speaking, Gen. 2:16; calling, Rom. 4:17; commanding, Isa. 5:6; rebuking (reproving, reprimanding), Pss. 18:16; 104:7; answering, Ps. 3:5; testifying, Mal. 2:14; resting, Gen. 2:2; working, John 5:17; seeing, Gen. 1:10; hearing, Ex. 2:24; smelling, Gen. 8:21; testing, Ps. 11:4, 5; sitting, Ps. 9:8; rising, Ps. 68:2; going, Ex. 34:9; coming, Ex. 25:22; walking, Lev. 26:12; descending, Gen. 11:5; meeting, Ex. 3:18; visiting, Gen. 21:1; passing over, Ex. 12:13; forsaking, Judg. 6:13; writing, Ex. 34:1; sealing, John 6:27; engraving, Isa. 49:16; striking, Isa. 11:4; chastising, Deut. 8:5; punishing, Job 5:17; binding up, Ps. 147:3; curing, Ps. 103:3; healing, Deut. 32:39; killing and making alive, Deut. 32:39; wiping away, Isa. 25:8; blotting out, 2 Kings 21:13; washing, Ps. 51:4; cleansing, Ps. 51:4; anointing, Ps. 2:6; adorning, Ezek. 16:11; clothing, Ps. 132:16; crowning, Ps. 8:6; girding, Ps. 18:33; destroying, Gen. 6:7; laying waste, Lev. 26:31; killing, Gen. 38:7; plaguing, Gen. 12:17; judging, Ps. 58:12; condemning, Job 10:2; and so forth. God is also very frequently designated by names that signify a certain occupation, office, relation, or relationship among human beings. He is a bridegroom, Isa. 61:10; a husband, Isa. 54:5; a father, Deut. 32:6; a judge, king, lawgiver, Isa. 33:22; a warrior, Ex. 15:3; a hero, Ps. 78:65; Zeph. 3:17; an artist and builder, Heb. 11:10; a husbandman, John 15:1; a shepherd, Ps. 23:1; a physician, Ex. 15:26; and so forth. In these capacities there is then again mention of his seat, throne, footstool, rod, scepter, weapons, bow, arrow, sword, shield, chariot, banner, book, seal, treasure, inheritance, and so forth. Indeed, in order to express what God is for his own, all manner of images are derived from animate and inanimate creation. He is compared to a lion, Isa. 31:4; an eagle, Deut. 32:11; a lamb, Isa. 53:7; a hen, Matt. 23:37; to the sun, Ps. 84:12; the morning star, Rev. 22:16; to a light, Ps. 27:1; a lamp, Rev. 21:23; a fire, Heb. 12:29; a spring or fountain, Ps. 36:10; a fountain of living water, Jer. 2:13; to food, bread, drink, water, ointment, Isa. 55:1; John 4:10; 6:35, 55; to a rock, Deut. 32:4; a hiding place, Ps. 119:114; a tower, Prov. 18:10; a high refuge, Ps. 9:10; a shadow, Pss. 91:1; 121:5; a shield, Ps. 84:12; a way, John 14:6; a temple, Rev. 21:22; and so forth. Polanus, _Synt. Theol._ II, c. 35; Alsted, _Theol. schol._, 104 sq.; Glassius, _Philol. Sacra_, 1691, pp. 1116–1181.
