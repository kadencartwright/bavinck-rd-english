# § 23. The Names of God

## 1.

All that becomes knowable to us of God in his revelation is designated in Holy Scripture by the name of God. The original meaning of the word שֵׁם is probably that of sign, identifying mark, σημα, _signum_, just as the Greek ὀνομα and the Latin _nomen_ are derived from the root _gno_ and thus designate that by which something is known, an identifying mark. A name is a sign of the one who bears it, a designation according to some property in which he reveals himself and becomes knowable. There is a connection between the name and its bearer, and this connection is not arbitrary but grounded in the bearer himself. Even among us, and still today, when names have mostly become sounds without meaning, this connection is still felt. A name is something personal and entirely different from a number or a specimen of its kind. It always feels more or less unpleasant when our name is written or used incorrectly. Our honor, our worth, our person and individuality are attached to our name. But this connection was much stronger in earlier times, when names still had a transparent meaning and were a revelation of the person or thing that received them. So it is also in Scripture. Adam had to give the animals names according to their nature, Gen. 2:19, 20. The meaning of many names in Scripture is given, together with the reason why they were given, for example Eve, Gen. 3:20; Cain, 4:1; Seth, 4:25; Noah, 5:29; Babel, 11:9; Ishmael, 16:11; Esau and Jacob, 25:25; Moses, Ex. 2:10; Jesus, Matt. 1:21; and so forth. Repeatedly a name is changed or a byname added when a person appears in another capacity: Abraham, Gen. 17:5; Sarah, 17:15; Israel, 32:28; Joshua, Num. 13:16; Jedidiah, 2 Sam. 12:25; Mara, Ruth 1:20; Peter, Mark 3:16; and so forth. After his ascension Christ received a name above every name, Phil. 2:9; Heb. 1:4, and in the new Jerusalem believers are given a new name, Rev. 2:17; 3:12; 22:4. The same applies to the name of God. There is an intimate connection between God and his name. According to Scripture this connection too is not accidental or arbitrary but established by God himself. Human beings do not name him; he gives himself a name. In the foreground, therefore, stands the name as revelation on God’s part, in the active and objective sense, as _nomen editum_. The name of God is then identical with the perfections he reveals outwardly, with his glory, Pss. 8:2; 72:19; his honor, Lev. 18:21; Pss. 86:11; 102:16; his delivering power, Ex. 15:3; Isa. 47:4; his service, Isa. 56:6; Jer. 23:27; his holiness, 1 Chron. 18:10; Pss. 105:3; 3:21; and so forth. The name is God himself as he reveals himself in one relation or another. That name, as revelation of God, is therefore great, Ezek. 36:23; holy, Ezek. 36:20; fearsome, Ps. 111:9; a high refuge, Ps. 20:2; a strong tower, Prov. 18:10. With proper names, specifically with the name YHWH, God made himself known to Israel. Through the Angel, in whose inward parts his name is, Ex. 23:20, he revealed himself to Israel. And through him he laid his name upon the children of Israel, Num. 6:27; established the memorial of his name among them, Ex. 20:24; set his name among them and caused it to dwell, Deut. 11:14; 12:5, particularly in the temple built for his name, 2 Sam. 7:13. His name now dwells in that temple, 2 Chron. 20:9; 33:4. Through that name he delivers, Ps. 54:3, and for the sake of that name he cannot abandon Israel, 1 Sam. 12:22; Isa. 48:9, 11; Pss. 31:4; 23:3; 143:11 f. But Israel therefore also may not blaspheme, profane, or use that name in vain, Ex. 20:7; Lev. 18:21; 24:11. On the contrary, that name must be called upon, recounted, magnified, known, confessed, feared, exalted, awaited, sought, and hallowed, Gen. 4:26; 12:2; Ex. 9:16; Deut. 28:58; 1 Kings 8:33; Pss. 5:12; 34:4; 52:11; 83:17; 122:4; Isa. 26:8; Matt. 6:9; John 12:28; and so forth. In the New Testament there is only one name through which God grants salvation; it is the name of Jesus Christ, Acts 4:12. But in that one name he has also revealed himself fully; it comprehends all that God is and will be for his own; in that name he communicates himself, with all his benefits, to his people, Matt. 28:19; Luke 24:47; John 20:31; Acts 2:38; 1 Cor. 6:11; James 5:14. And believers are to expect still richer revelation in the new Jerusalem, Rev. 3:12, when his name will be upon everyone’s forehead, Rev. 22:4. In Holy Scripture the name of God designates not God as he exists in himself but God in his revelation, in his manifold relations to creatures. This name, however, is not arbitrary; God reveals himself so and in that manner because he is who he is. His honor, his fame, all his perfections, his entire revelation, his own divine being, are therefore attached to his name. But for this reason that name grants special privileges to the one to whom it is revealed and at the same time imposes distinctive obligations upon him. The name of God implies that, as he reveals himself in it, he is now also named accordingly by creatures. The _nomen editum_ passes over into the _nomen inditum_. The εἰναι and καλεισθαι are two sides of the same reality in Holy Scripture. God is the one he is called, and he is called the one he is. What he reveals of himself is expressed and reproduced in definite names. He grants his creatures the privilege of naming and addressing him on the ground of and in agreement with his revelation. The one name of God, as the sum of his entire revelation in both nature and grace, divides for us into many, many names. Only in this way do we gain an overview of the riches of his revelation and the profound meaning of his name. We name him and may name him according to all that becomes manifest of his essence in creation and re-creation. But all those names, as designations of God, impose upon us the obligation to hallow and glorify them. It is the one name, the full revelation, and to that extent God’s own essence itself, with which we have to do in all those names. Through his name God places himself in a definite relation to us; to this we ought to respond in our relation to him. Schultz, _Altt. Theol._⁴ 513 f.; Oehler, _Theol. des A. T._ §56; art. “Name” in Herzog²; Smend, _Lehrb._, p. 281 f.; Cremer, s.v. ὀνομα.
