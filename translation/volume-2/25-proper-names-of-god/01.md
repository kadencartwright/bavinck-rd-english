# § 25. The Proper Names of God

## 1.

Among the names of God, those names by which he is designated, named, and addressed as a proper personal being in distinction from all creatures clearly stand apart in the first place. All languages possess such names for the divine being. Although God has no name in himself, we need to designate him and have no other means for doing so than a name. _Nisi enim nomen scieris, rerum cognitio perit_, Isidore in Moor, I 504. The Greek word θεος, formerly derived from τιθεναι, θεειν, θεασθαι, Suicerus, s.v.; M. Vitringa, I 134, is connected today by some philologists with Ζευς, Διος, Jupiter, Deus, Diana, Juno, Dio, Dieu; it is then identical with the Sanskrit name _dêva_, bright heaven, and derived from the root _div_, to shine, to glitter. The interchange of the designation heaven and God occurs in many languages. The oldest Greek god was called Uranus, certainly identical with the Sanskrit name Varuna; _Taengri_ among the Tatars and Turks and _Thian_ among the Chinese designate both heaven and God; and in Scripture too the name God is replaced by that of heaven, for example in the expression kingdom of heaven or of God. Another Greek name is δαιμων, from the verb δαιω, and designates God as distributor and disposer of fate; κυριος, by contrast, from κυρος, means the mighty one, lord, owner, ruler. Our word _God_ is of uncertain origin. It has been connected with the word _good_; with the Zend _khodâ_, _a se datus, per se ipsum existens_; with the Sanskrit _gudha_ or _gutha_, κευδω, which would designate God as the hidden one; or with a root, κοδω, κοσμος, which would mean to order, arrange; or with the Aryan _çuddhas_, pure, good, and so forth, but all these derivations are uncertain. The name Asura among the Indians and Ahura among the Persians designates God as the living one. Cf. Kluge, _Etymol. Wörterbuch der deutschen Sprache_, s.v. “Gott”; Cremer, _Wört. der neut. Gr._, s.v.; Herzog² 5, 290; Hoekstra, _Wijs. Godsd._ I 309 f.
