## 7.

Finally, Holy Scripture also gives us some insight into God’s immanent relations through the name of the Holy Spirit. It deserves notice beforehand that the doctrine of the Holy Spirit is the same throughout all the Scriptures of Old and New Covenants. Although revealed much more clearly in the New Testament, it is also present in principle in the Old. The New Testament is conscious of giving no other doctrine of the Spirit than that found in the Old Testament. It was the same Holy Spirit who formerly spoke through the prophets, Matt. 22:43; Mark 12:36; Acts 1:16; 28:25; Heb. 3:7; 10:15; 1 Pet. 1:11; 2 Pet. 1:21; testified in the days of Noah, 1 Pet. 3:19; was resisted by Israel, Acts 7:51; worked faith, 2 Cor. 4:13; and would descend upon the Messiah and dwell in the church, Matt. 12:18; Luke 4:18, 19; Acts 2:16. Now although the divine being is Spirit, John 4:24, and is holy, Isa. 6:3, the Holy Spirit is nevertheless in Scripture the designation of a particular person in the divine being, distinguished from Father and Son. He bears this name because of his particular _modus subsistendi_. Spirit properly means wind, breath. The Holy Spirit is the breath of the Almighty, Job 33:4, the Spirit of his mouth, Ps. 33:6, who is compared by Jesus to the wind, John 3:8, and is breathed upon his disciples, John 20:22; cf. 2 Thess. 2:8. The Spirit is God as the immanent principle of life in everything created. And he is called holy because he himself stands in a particular relation to God and places all things in a particular relation to God. He is not the spirit of a human being or of a creature, but the Spirit of God, the Holy Spirit, Ps. 51:13; Isa. 63:10, 11. Just as breath proceeds from our mouth, so the Spirit proceeds from God and keeps every creature alive. Therefore he is called the Spirit of God, the Spirit of the Lord, the Spirit of the Father, Gen. 1:2; Isa. 11:2; Matt. 10:20, and also the Spirit of Christ, the Spirit of the Son, Rom. 8:2, 9; 1 Cor. 2:6; 2 Cor. 3:17, 18; Phil. 1:19; Gal. 3:2; 4:6; 1 Pet. 1:11, standing before the throne of God and of the Lamb, Rev. 1:4; 3:1; 4:5; 5:6. This procession of the Spirit is designated in Scripture by various names. Usually it is expressed by saying that the Spirit is given by God or by Christ, Num. 11:29; Neh. 9:20; Isa. 42:1; Ezek. 36:27; John 3:34; 1 John 3:24; 4:13; sent or sent forth, Ps. 104:30; John 14:26; 15:26; 16:7; Gal. 4:6; Rev. 5:6; poured or poured out, Isa. 32:15; 44:3; Joel 2:28; Zech. 12:10; Acts 2:17, 18; descended from God, Matt. 3:16; placed in the midst of Israel, Isa. 63:11; Hag. 2:6; laid upon someone, Matt. 12:18; breathed upon someone, John 20:22; and so forth. But it is also said that the Spirit proceeds, ἐκπορευεται παρα του πατρος, John 15:26. This took place particularly on the day of Pentecost. Therefore the personality of the Holy Spirit now first appears clearly. In the Old Testament there is indeed a distinction between God and his Spirit. But the nature of that distinction remains obscure. Οἰπω γαρ ἠν πνευμα, ὁτι Ιησους οὐδεπω ἐδοξασθη, John 7:39. But now he is spoken of as a person. He is designated as εκεινος, John 15:26; 16:13, 14; παρακλητος, John 15:26; cf. 1 John 2:1; ἀλλος παρακλητος, John 14:16; he speaks of himself in the first person, Acts 13:2; and all kinds of personal faculties and activities are ascribed to him, such as searching, 1 Cor. 2:10, 11; judging, Acts 15:28; hearing, John 16:13; speaking, Acts 13:2; Rev. 2:7; etc.; 14:13; 22:17; willing, 1 Cor. 12:11; teaching, John 14:26; praying, Rom. 8:27; testifying, John 15:26; and so forth. And he is coordinated with Father and Son and placed on the same level, Matt. 28:19; 1 Cor. 12:4–6; 2 Cor. 13:13; Rev. 1:4. Now this cannot be unless he is also true God. Just as his personality, so too the deity of the Holy Spirit first comes clearly to light in the New Testament. It appears first of all from the fact that, despite the distinction existing between God and his Spirit, it is nevertheless entirely the same whether God or his Spirit says something, dwells in us, or is despised by us, Isa. 6:9 and Acts 28:25; Jer. 31:31 and Heb. 10:15; Ps. 95:7, 8 and Heb. 3:7–9; Acts 5:3 and 4; Rom. 8:9 and 10; 1 Cor. 3:16 and 6:19; Eph. 3:22. This comes fully into its own only when unity of being accompanies personal distinction. Further, all kinds of divine attributes are ascribed just as much to God’s Spirit as to God himself, for example, eternity, Heb. 9:14; omnipresence, Ps. 139:7; omniscience, 1 Cor. 2:10, 11; omnipotence, 1 Cor. 12:4–6; and this again presupposes that the Spirit is one in being with God himself. The same is the case with the divine works of creation, Gen. 1:2; Ps. 33:6; Job 33:4; Ps. 104:30, and of re-creation. Indeed, in the latter especially his deity comes clearly to expression. He is the one who by his anointing equipped Christ for his office, Isa. 11:2; 61:1 and Luke 4:18; Isa. 42:1 and Matt. 12:18; Luke 1:35; Matt. 3:16; 4:1; John 3:34; Matt. 12:28; Heb. 9:14; Rom. 1:4; who equips the apostles for their special task, Matt. 10:20; Luke 12:12; 21:15; 24:49; John 14:16 f.; 15:26; 16:13 f.; etc.; who distributes all kinds of gifts and powers to believers, 1 Cor. 12:4–11; and who above all causes the fullness of Christ to dwell in the church. The Holy Spirit stands in the same relation to Christ as Christ does to the Father. Just as the Son has and does and speaks nothing from himself but receives everything from the Father, John 5:26; 16:15, so the Holy Spirit takes everything from Christ, John 16:13, 14. Just as the Son testifies of the Father and glorifies the Father, John 1:18; 17:4, 6, so in turn the Holy Spirit testifies of and glorifies the Son, 15:26; 16:14. Just as no one comes to the Father except through the Son, Matt. 11:27; John 14:6, so no one can say that Jesus is Lord except through the Holy Spirit, 1 Cor. 12:3. There is no fellowship with God except through the Spirit. But that Spirit then also grants all the benefits Christ acquired: regeneration, John 3:3; conviction of sin, John 16:8–11; adoption, Rom. 8:15; renewal, Titus 3:5; the love of God, Rom. 5:5; all kinds of spiritual fruits, Gal. 5:22; sealing, Rom. 8:23; 2 Cor. 1:22; 5:5; Eph. 1:13; 4:30; resurrection, Rom. 8:10. Indeed, through the Spirit we have direct, immediate fellowship with none less than the Son and the Father themselves. The Holy Spirit is God himself in us, John 14:23 f.; 1 Cor. 3:16; 6:19; 2 Cor. 6:16; Gal. 2:20; Col. 3:11; Eph. 3:17; Phil. 1:8, 21. Who can grant us all this, who can cause God himself to dwell in our hearts, except one who is himself God? Therefore divine honor is also due him. He stands alongside Father and Son as cause of all salvation, Matt. 28:19; 1 Cor. 12:4–6; 2 Cor. 13:13; Rev. 1:4. In his name we are baptized, Matt. 28:19. From him are all life and power. He is the author of our prayers, Zech. 12:10; Rom. 8:15, 16. And over against this the church is warned not to grieve him, Isa. 63:10; Eph. 4:30; indeed, blasphemy against the Holy Spirit is unforgivable, Matt. 12:31, 32.
